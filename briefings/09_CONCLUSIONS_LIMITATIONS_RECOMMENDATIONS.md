# Briefing 09: Expected Outputs, Limitations, and Next Work

**Proposal stage: this is not a final-study conclusion.** The legacy filename is retained for compatibility.

## Intended outputs

The planned study will deliver the specified architecture, a reproducible evaluation engine, matched reference-based accuracy/selection/cost evidence, and an account of dependence and applicability limits. The supporting prototype will make the computation inspectable. Separate domain cases will document defensible mappings.

The empirical result may favor GBBRPM in some regimes, show equivalence, or show that alternatives select better. The diagnostic may improve or worsen agreement. No winner, empirical ranking advantage, successful correction, or operational benefit is presumed.

| Runtime/memory tradeoff against matched methods | Planned cost measurements; historical timings are preliminary |

| Practical usefulness or event prediction | Requires independent case-specific outcomes |

## Main limitations

The core excludes cycles, feedback, physical-time evolution, and full hydraulic/electrical dynamics. Domain scores are not calibrated probabilities or comparable physical units across cases. Shared ancestry is not corrected by bounded aggregation. Risk outputs alone do not separately identify S and tau. Receiving-state scores do not estimate intervention benefit.

The benchmark's independent primitives define a constructed process, not universal propagation. Restricted trees and lineage grouping have different guarantees and costs. Monte Carlo can leave low-probability rankings unresolved. A feasible compute allocation must be established before committing to the full inventory.

Earlier N1–N5 fixtures are unavailable for direct identity checking; reconstructed inputs retain that label. Express originals are included and verified. Electrical loading is real but injected disturbances are controlled. Operational drainage and event-aligned electrical outcomes remain evidence needs. Historical SWMM disagreement limits hydraulic claims.

## Next work in dependency order

1. Confirm the scoped proposal, required prior-art comparisons, milestones, and compute allocation.
2. Implement and validate exact/Monte Carlo references, each comparator, and reporting rules on development cases.
3. Pilot compute, finalize eligibility/manifests and any feasibility amendment, then freeze before held-out outcomes.
4. Run evaluation graphs, precision audits, and separate heterogeneous/Express analyses; retain negative, tied, and unresolved cases.
5. Complete cost measurements, reviewed historical reruns, eligible domain analyses, and recorded prototype verification.
6. Write results and conclusions from that evidence. Seek independent domain outcomes for practical claims.
