**Present the research question and planned evidence. Do not use the earlier full-study results narrative.**

| Order | Main point | Source or visual |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current title, DAG scope, model/engine distinction | Chapter 1 and title metadata |

Allocate the most explanation to the question, reference semantics, matched controls, and evaluation design. A source trace can clarify the formula, but replay polish is secondary to the scientific plan.

## Useful verbal transitions

“Bounded output specifies a mathematical behavior; the next question is whether it preserves useful receiving-node ordering.”

“The benchmark assigns probability semantics to its own primitive events. The domain adapters keep their index interpretation.”

“The bracket establishes the direction of score slack. The experiments will measure its tightness and its consequences for selection.”

“The three cases examine mappings and evidence limits. Operational usefulness still requires an independent outcome.”

## Preliminary evidence slide

Keep it separate from the proposed benchmark. Label historical 136-run behavior evidence, unfavorable SWMM agreement, Express index calculations, and the electrical 43-scenario case as preliminary. Use only current Appendix D values if a numerical example is necessary. Do not combine these counts with the new inventory or call their output agreement ground-truth accuracy.

## Demonstration boundary

Use a recorded prototype/engine revision and a validated small fixture. Explain source B, incoming Q, current R, and settled ranking. Do not present animation time as physical time. Do not claim the known input-validation issue or pending browser checks are resolved without matching-source evidence.

## Closing statement

“The planned study will determine when this scalar DAG recursion provides useful receiving-state selection under the declared process, what dependence and cost limits it has, and how far the supporting domain evidence allows its interpretation.”

Use future tense for experiments and expected outputs, present tense for definitions/propositions, and past tense only for explicitly historical exploratory work. Use source sections rather than old page counts; confirm institutional dates and front matter separately.
