\vspace{3\baselineskip}
\noindent Network-state assessment requires distinguishing local disturbance from influence inherited through connected entities. This proposal will specify, implement, and evaluate the Graph-Based Bounded Risk Propagation Model (GBBRPM) for comparative receiving-state prioritization on directed acyclic graphs. The model will combine local disturbance with relation-modified upstream states through the established probabilistic-sum operator. Domain outputs will remain non-probabilistic indices. A separately defined stochastic activation benchmark will provide exact or uncertainty-qualified reference probabilities for evaluating the same recursion under explicit independent-primitive assumptions. The methodology will examine conditional score bounds, shared ancestry, attenuation, graph structure, fixed-budget node selection, and computational cost. Matched comparisons will include local-only scoring, alternative bounded aggregators, a recursion ablation, a restricted-tree comparator, and an ancestry-aware diagnostic. Generated and controlled graph families will support development and evaluation under a frozen protocol. Drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network cases will separately assess parameter mappings, DAG eligibility, and evidence limitations. A versioned evaluation engine and interactive prototype will expose inputs, contributions, rankings, and optional trace replay. Preliminary calculations and exploratory findings will document feasibility and limitations rather than serve as final evaluation results. The planned study will establish an evidence-based account of mechanism agreement and accuracy--cost tradeoffs; operational usefulness will require independent domain outcomes. The proposal does not assume that a simpler core, bounded output, or coherent instantiation establishes predictive validity.

\vspace{\baselineskip}
\noindent Keywords: risk propagation, directed acyclic graphs, bounded aggregation, shared ancestry, comparative prioritization
