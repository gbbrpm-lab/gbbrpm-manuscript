# Adviser Briefing: Current GBBRPM Proposal

Aligned to the uploaded Chapters I–III on 15 October 2026. **Benchmark experiments are planned, not completed.**

## One-minute explanation

GBBRPM is the proposed Graph-Based Bounded Risk Propagation Model for receiving-state prioritization on DAGs. It combines local disturbance with modified current upstream states through the established probabilistic-sum operator. The study will evaluate when that inexpensive scalar recursion preserves useful receiving-node selection, how uncertain shared origins affect its reference agreement, and what accuracy–cost tradeoffs alternatives provide. A separately declared independent-primitive cascade will supply exact or uncertainty-qualified activation references. Domain outputs remain indices. Drainage, software dependencies, and electrical networks support separate mappings and evidence-limit analyses. Existing exploratory calculations motivate the design; they are not the new benchmark's results.

## Mathematical claim and empirical question

Under the specified benchmark assumptions, `M<=T<=pi<=R<=H` and `0<=R-pi<=R-T<=R-M`. M is recursive maximum; T is the exact marginal on an unthresholded, maximum-path-preserving target tree; R is GBBRPM; H is recursive clipped sum. The proposition specializes established arguments. It is not a new correlation inequality or a calibration of domain scores.

The experiment will measure how tight those bounds are and whether the resulting rankings select high-reference-state nodes at fixed budgets. A better score bound need not improve ordering. The ancestry-grouping diagnostic may discard legitimate opportunities and is not presumed to correct the recursion.

## Prospective evidence

560 matched branch scenarios; 3,600 uniform generated-DAG scenarios with 720 development and 2,880 evaluation cases; 144 secondary heterogeneous scenarios with 24 development and 120 evaluation cases; 12 controlled Express-topology scenarios; and 48 additional precision solves on existing generated cases. These counts are prospective, with feasibility amendments frozen before held-out outcomes.

Compare local-only, recursive maximum, clipped addition, GBBRPM, local-upstream ablation, restricted trees, the published Abbe et al. (2020) DAG lower method, and the specified ancestry diagnostic. The full published upper method is not a required full-grid implementation. Report fixed-budget capture/regret, uncertainty-resolved ordering, score tightness, and matched runtime/memory.
