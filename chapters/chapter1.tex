\section{Introduction}
\hyphenation{GBBRPM}

\subsection{Background of the Study}\label{background-of-the-study}

Network propagation models represent connected entities and the relations
through which one entity can influence another. Across applications, the
propagated quantity may be activation, compromise, disturbance, or risk,
and its meaning determines the appropriate update rule
\citep{kempe2003,munoz2017,faraji2026}. A local observation and a receiving
node's accumulated state are consequently different analytical quantities.
When a model includes inherited influence, nodes with similar local inputs
can acquire different receiving states because their predecessors and
relation conditions differ. Comparative assessment therefore requires an
explicit account of both the local state and the permitted network influence.

Several modeling choices shape this account. Graph topology determines
which influences can reach a node; edge modifiers determine how strongly
upstream state contributes; recursion determines whether inherited state
continues along later relations; and aggregation determines how local and
incoming terms combine. These choices can change magnitudes and rankings.
Structural position, accumulated receiving state, influence exerted by a
source, and benefit from intervention are distinct targets. An evaluation
must identify its target before treating a high score as useful priority.

The mathematical components relevant to bounded propagation already have
established precedents. The probabilistic-sum t-conorm combines bounded
inputs through complement product \citep{klement2004}. Leaky noisy-OR uses
related background-plus-parent algebra in a probabilistic setting
\citep{fallet2012}, while RIPA propagates current risk through relation and
receiver modifiers with additive aggregation \citep{faraji2026}. MIA restricts
cascade calculations to local influence trees, and association-based cascade
bounds provide another precedent for analyzing approximation
\citep{chen2010mia,abbe2017,abbe2020}. The research value of selecting these mechanisms
will therefore depend on a justified formulation and comparative evidence.
Boundedness alone establishes neither correct receiving-state rankings nor
practical usefulness.

Shared ancestry provides a specific condition for investigating these
choices. Incoming contributions may originate from different local sources
or from one origin whose influence splits and reconverges. Separate
aggregation of those terms can produce different results depending on the
assumed process and dependence structure. Restricted-tree methods and
cascade bounds explicitly address related approximation questions
\citep{chen2010mia,abbe2017,abbe2020}. The present study will examine how source
uncertainty, attenuation, and graph structure affect the selected recursion's
reference agreement and priority ordering, including regimes where simpler
rules or dependence treatments perform equally well or better.

To address these architectural and evaluative questions systematically, the
proponents designed the \textbf{Graph-Based Bounded Risk Propagation Model
(GBBRPM)} as a modular adapter-to-core framework on \textbf{directed acyclic
graphs (DAGs)}, together with a \textbf{Two-Stage Deterministic Certified
Top-$k$ Screening} harness and a traceable \textbf{GBBRPM evaluation engine}.
Rather than claiming a newly invented algebraic operator, the proponents
constructed this framework by integrating the established complement-product
aggregation primitive \citep{klement2004} with decoupled domain adapters,
fail-closed input and acyclicity validation, and a stochastic-bracket
evaluation harness. Its core combines local disturbance with modified current
upstream states to produce a dimensionless receiving-state index in $[0,1]$.
The core equations remain shared across domains, while the meanings and
construction of local disturbance, susceptibility, and transmission remain
explicitly separated inside each domain adapter. The proponent-designed
evaluation engine implements the versioned contract and exposes input,
contribution, and ranking traces through an interactive research prototype.

The primary controlled evaluation will use a separately defined stochastic
activation process with independent initial and conditional edge events.
This construction will supply exact or uncertainty-qualified reference
activation probabilities for matched comparisons. Under those assumptions,
the GBBRPM recursion has a conditional nodewise upper-bound interpretation;
Chapter~3 states its receiving-state bracket, formal assumptions, proof, and
relation to established bounding arguments. Built directly on that bracket
without altering the core equations, Two-Stage Deterministic Certified
Top-$k$ Screening and the zero-cost scalar bracket width $W_j = R_j - M_j$
will be evaluated alongside matched comparators for bound tightness,
fixed-budget receiving-node selection, deterministic certification rate, and
computational cost. This benchmark characterizes the mechanism under its
specified process; domain-index inputs retain their own meanings, and
practical usefulness requires independent outcomes appropriate to each domain
task.

Urban drainage supplied the original practical motivation. Camera-based
blockage assessment provides local condition information, while graph-based
critical-pipe and weakest-link analyses evaluate connected-system behavior
\citep{vandaele2024,dastgir2023,meijer2023}. These approaches illustrate why
local condition and network-context assessment deserve separate attention.
Through an official Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Freedom of
Information (FOI) release (\texttt{FOI-REQ-2026-7383};
\citealp{dpwh2026foi,upplanades2016}), the study verified an eligible
real-world Cagayan de Oro urban drainage reach DAG
(\texttt{drainage\_\allowbreak dpwh\_\allowbreak cdo\_\allowbreak bitanag\_\allowbreak kolambog\_\allowbreak v1}: 11 nodes, 11 directed
edges, engineering susceptibilities $S_{ij} \in [0.4639, 0.8471]$, Manning
conveyance checks within $1\%$, and real shared-ancestry reconvergence at the
Agora confluence \texttt{J\_AGORA\_CONF} / PCSWMM \texttt{J5}), which passes
structural instantiation eligibility (Gate~A) using Tier-2 controlled
blockage injections while retaining agency flood-hotspot reports as
exploratory context only (Gate~B fail). The proposal examines this drainage
reach alongside software-dependency (\texttt{Express 4.18.2}) and
electrical-network (\texttt{Caltech SoCal 28-Bus} Component~2;
\citealp{xie2025}) instantiations of the common core to evaluate defensible
mappings, DAG eligibility, and evidence limits. Three instantiations do not
establish universal applicability, and their scores are never pooled onto a
common physical risk scale. Preliminary worked calculations and exploratory
findings are retained in the appendices; the evaluation protocols are
specified in Chapter~3.

\subsection{Statement of the Problem}\label{statement-of-the-problem}

The central problem is to determine when GBBRPM's complement-product
recursion gives useful receiving-state rankings under a declared reference
process, how shared ancestry limits that agreement, to what extent its
analytic bracket enables deterministic top-$k$ certification and self-diagnosed
reliability, and what computational tradeoffs accompany alternative mechanisms.
Existing aggregation, restricted-tree approximation, and cascade-bounding
methods establish relevant precedents
\citep{klement2004,chen2010mia,abbe2017,abbe2020}. The proposed study will
therefore assess the proponent-designed architecture against matched
comparators rather than infer its contribution from a new name, a bounded
formula, or different scores alone.

The controlled comparison will hold graph and primitive inputs fixed across
methods while varying predeclared source, attenuation, and topology conditions.
Maximum and clipped-additive aggregation, local-only scoring, a recursion
ablation, a restricted-tree comparator, the published DAG lower bound of
\citet{abbe2020}, and the ancestry-aware diagnostic will provide distinct
comparison conditions. The analytic benchmark bracket will establish nodewise
score bounds under independent primitive events and support Two-Stage
Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening; the experiments will determine
bound tightness, certification coverage, bracket-width reliability, and the
resulting selection quality. A tighter score bound will not automatically
imply a better ordering.

This evaluation will address a receiving-state task: accumulated local and
inherited influence at candidate nodes. Its stochastic reference will define
activation probabilities only within the constructed process. Domain indices
will not be relabeled as event probabilities, and a high receiving-state
score will not be interpreted as source influence or intervention benefit.
Drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network cases will assess
coherent instantiation separately; any practical prioritization claim will
require independent evidence relevant to that case's task.

The intended lightweight design will be assessed through matched runtime and
memory measurements, required inputs, and preprocessing costs. A single-pass
DAG core can have limited computational requirements while still omitting
important dependence or physical mechanisms. The study will investigate
that accuracy--cost tradeoff and report unfavorable or unresolved findings.
It will address the following research questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item How can local disturbance, current upstream state, relation-specific
modifiers, and bounded aggregation be designed and formally specified into
GBBRPM for comparative node-risk prioritization on DAGs?
\item How can the proposed formulation be implemented reproducibly in a
GBBRPM evaluation engine and presented in a prototype that preserves
fail-closed input validation and computational traceability?
\item Under an explicitly specified stochastic cascade reference, how do
GBBRPM, maximum aggregation, clipped additive aggregation, local-only
scoring, a local-upstream recursion ablation, a restricted-tree
comparator, and Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening differ in
bound tightness, certification coverage, and fixed-budget node selection?
\item How do shared ancestry, source uncertainty, and attenuation affect
the receiving-state bracket's tightness, scalar width reliability
($W_j = R_j - M_j$), and ranking quality; when does an ancestry-aware
diagnostic improve or worsen them; and what mathematical and robustness
properties characterize the frozen core?
\item What runtime, memory, and input requirements accompany GBBRPM,
Two-Stage Certified Top-$k$ Screening, the matched comparators, and the
reference solvers at stated precision?
\item Can the same core be instantiated coherently in drainage,
software-dependency, and electrical-network cases, and what applicability
limits follow from each case's data and evaluation?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Objectives of the Study}\label{objectives-of-the-study}

\subsubsection{General Objective}\label{general-objective}

The general objective is to design, specify, implement, and evaluate GBBRPM
for bounded receiving-state prioritization on directed acyclic graphs,
determine its reference accuracy, deterministic screening effectiveness, and
computational tradeoffs under controlled aggregation and dependence
conditions, and establish the evidence limits of its drainage,
software-dependency, and electrical-network instantiations. Within this
objective, \emph{design} refers to the proponent-constructed modular
adapter-to-core architecture, Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$
Screening harness, and traceable software workflow, whereas \emph{specify}
refers to the formal mathematical contract governing graph admissibility,
bounded parameter semantics, recursive equations, analytical assumptions, and
the stochastic-bracket evaluation protocol.

\subsubsection{Specific Objectives}\label{specific-objectives}

Specifically, the study aims to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item design and formally specify GBBRPM by defining its modular
adapter-to-core architecture, DAG representation, bounded state semantics,
local disturbance, relation-specific modifiers, recursive update rule,
aggregation mechanism, formal assumptions, and expected mathematical
properties;
\item implement a reproducible deterministic evaluation engine and an
interactive research prototype that will enforce fail-closed input validation,
execute the same versioned core, and expose traceable node states, edge
contributions, bracket widths, and rankings;
\item evaluate receiving-state error, fixed-budget node selection, and
Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening against exact or
uncertainty-qualified stochastic references using matched aggregation
comparators, a recursion ablation, a restricted-tree comparator, and a
published DAG lower bound, while separately characterizing
heterogeneous-input effects and saturation;
\item characterize shared-origin dependence under controlled source and
attenuation changes, evaluate both the zero-cost bracket-width reliability
indicator ($W_j = R_j - M_j$) and an ancestry-aware diagnostic against the
same reference, formalize the benchmark bracket with its assumptions, and
establish the frozen core's analytical properties, boundary behavior,
sensitivity, and perturbation robustness;
\item characterize the evaluation engine's and certified screening harness's
computational and input requirements through complexity analysis, runtime and
memory measurements, and a documented comparison of required information and
modeled mechanisms; and
\item assess whether the same core formulation can be instantiated
coherently in the three selected domains through documented DAG
construction, parameter mappings, within-domain experiments, and explicit
evidence and applicability limits.
\end{enumerate}

Literature synthesis will inform the design requirements and interpretation
of these objectives. The principal research outputs will be the designed and
specified architecture, comparative evaluation evidence, certified screening
results, and an explicit account of accuracy, cost, and applicability
conditions. The evaluation engine and prototype will support reproducibility
and inspection of those outputs.

\subsubsection{Research Hypotheses and Measurable Evaluation Criteria}
\label{research-hypotheses}

To maintain a clear boundary between mathematical deduction and empirical
testing, the study strictly separates \emph{proven mathematical invariants}
from \emph{falsifiable empirical hypotheses}. Under the formal contract and
Assumptions~A1--A5 detailed in Chapter~3, boundedness ($R_j \in [0,1]$), local
and global monotonicity with respect to independently increased $B$, $S$, or
$\tau$ on a fixed DAG, and the conditional independent-primitive
receiving-state bracket $M_j \le T_j \le \pi_j \le R_j \le H_j$ follow
analytically by topological induction and Harris--FKG positive association.
These invariants are therefore enforced as zero-tolerance formal verification
checks in automated tests rather than framed as uncertain experimental
hypotheses; any discrepancy against an exact solver signals an implementation
or assumption violation.

By contrast, the empirical benchmark tests five falsifiable research
hypotheses ($H_1$--$H_5$) across the predeclared controlled scenario suite,
retaining null, equivalent, and unfavorable regimes honestly:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{H\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Bracket Tightness and Dependence Scaling.} Under the
independent-primitive stochastic reference, the one-sided GBBRPM upper slack
$R_j - \pi_j$ and tree-refined bracket width $R_j - T_j$ will remain small in
low-reconvergence or high-attenuation regimes, but will increase
systematically with overlapping shared-origin ancestry, intermediate source
uncertainty ($0 < b < 1$), and higher effective edge weights $w_{ij}$. In the
matched branch controls, $R_j - \pi_j$ will be strictly larger under uncertain
shared origins ($0 < b < 1$) than under matched independent origins (where
$R_j = \pi_j$), while collapsing to zero when the shared source is certain
($b = 1$).
\item \textbf{Fixed-Budget Top-$k$ Selection Regret vs.\ Comparators.} Over
the candidate set $C = \{j \in V : B_j = 0\}$ at fixed budgets
$f \in \{0.10, 0.20, 0.30\}$, GBBRPM will achieve lower or comparable
fixed-budget selection regret ($\operatorname{Regret}_k$) and higher or
comparable captured activation mass ($\operatorname{Capture}(\mathcal{T}_k)$)
relative to local-only scoring, the local-upstream recursion ablation
($R_j^{\mathrm{local\text{-}upstream}}$), and recursive clipped-additive
aggregation ($H_j$, especially under multi-source saturation), while exposing
measurable accuracy--distortion tradeoffs against recursive maximum ($M_j$),
the target-specific restricted in-arborescence ($T_j$), the DAG lower bound
of \citet{abbe2020}, and the ancestry-aware diagnostic---including
high-overlap regimes where unequal upper slack produces interval-resolved
pairwise rank reversals.
\item \textbf{Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Resolution and
Speedup.} Exploiting $M_j \le T_j \le \pi_j \le R_j$, Two-Stage Deterministic
Certified Top-$k$ Screening (Stage~1 scalar $[M_j, R_j]$ filtering in
$O(|V|+|E|)$ followed by Stage~2 targeted $T_j$ refinement restricted to the
uncertified boundary band $U_k^{(1)}$) will deterministically resolve a
substantial fraction of candidate top-$k$ memberships
$(|I_k^{(2)}| + |O_k^{(2)}|)/|C|$ with zero Monte Carlo trials and zero false
certifications against exact $\pi_j$, shrink the uncertified boundary band
($|U_k^{(2)}| \le |U_k^{(1)}|$), and deliver substantial wall-clock speedup
relative to both full-graph $T_j$ computation and $N = 100{,}000$-trial Monte
Carlo estimation.
\item \textbf{Self-Diagnosing Bracket-Width Reliability ($W_j = R_j - M_j$).}
Across synthetic DAG families, weights, and source regimes, the zero-cost
scalar bracket width $W_j = R_j - M_j$---computable in a single $O(|V|+|E|)$
pass without oracle knowledge of $\pi_j$---will exhibit strong positive
association with both actual nodewise slack $R_j - \pi_j$ and regime-level
interval-resolved reversal incidence, thereby serving as a computable
reliability indicator for flagging high-distortion cases that warrant Stage~2
or Monte Carlo escalation.
\item \textbf{Near-Linear Computational Scaling.} On prepared sparse DAGs
($|V| \in \{20, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1000\}$, $|E| \approx 3|V|$) and denser
stress DAGs ($|V| \le 250$, $|E| = \lfloor 0.25|V|(|V|-1)/2\rfloor$),
single-scenario GBBRPM validation and propagation will scale near-linearly in
$|V|+|E|$ with $O(|V|+|E|)$ memory and orders-of-magnitude lower runtime than
$N=100{,}000$-trial Monte Carlo estimation and full-graph $T_j$ construction,
with complete ranking contributing only the expected $O(|V|\log|V|)$ sorting
overhead.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Significance of the Study}\label{significance-of-the-study}

\subsubsection{Theoretical Significance}

The study designs and formally specifies an architecture for combining
local disturbance with recursively propagated network context on DAGs.
The proposed methodological significance lies in a reference-based
account of when the adopted recursion gives accurate receiving-state estimates
under stated stochastic assumptions, when its analytic bracket enables
zero-Monte-Carlo deterministic top-$k$ certification and self-diagnosed
reliability, how shared origins affect reference agreement, and what
computational tradeoffs accompany alternative rules. Basic operator
properties and the architecture's name will not stand as the original result.
The declared index semantics remain separate from benchmark probabilities.
Under the synthetic independent-primitive process, the same GBBRPM recursion
serves as a nodewise upper bound; that conditional mathematical interpretation
does not calibrate domain inputs. Existing association-based cascade bounds
are explicitly compared before any novelty claim \citep{abbe2017,abbe2020}.
The study distinguishes properties established analytically by the formulation
from empirical claims that require computational or domain evidence, including
the difference between bounded aggregation and correction for shared-origin
dependence.

Existing propagation and risk literature supplies relevant mechanisms
\citep{kempe2003,buldyrev2010,sheyner2002,munoz2017,petrone2018}. The proposed
contribution will be assessed through the design, specification, and evaluation
of the selected comparative-state task. The adopted t-conorm and its
basic properties are credited as established mathematics
\citep{klement2004}; their use or rederivation is not claimed as
a new aggregation theory. A common interface for domain-specific inputs allows
architectural reuse to be examined without assuming common physical semantics
or universal applicability. Susceptibility and transmission retain
distinct conceptual roles, while their separate empirical identification
is not inferred from outputs that depend on their product.

\subsubsection{Practical Significance}

The evaluation engine and prototype will provide an inspectable workflow for
examining local inputs, inherited contributions, scalar reliability widths,
and resulting receiving-state rankings. The comparison will characterize how
the simple scalar recursion, Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$
Screening, and alternative mechanisms differ in benchmark agreement,
deterministic certification coverage, and selected priorities. Runtime,
memory, and input requirements will make the cost of those choices explicit.

The drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network cases will document
how the common architecture can be instantiated and where the available
information restricts its interpretation. Potential screening or inspection
uses will depend on defensible parameter construction and independent
outcome evidence within each domain. Lower cost, plausible ordering, and
coherent mappings do not alone establish maintenance benefit, exploitation
prediction, or fault prediction. Operational decision claims remain strictly
limited to the tasks and outcomes actually evaluated.

\subsubsection{Expected Contributions}\label{expected-contributions}

To prevent ambiguity between established mathematical primitives and original
work, the study delineates its expected contributions across three distinct
layers:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Credited Mathematical Primitives (Established Prior Art).}
The complement-product update rule is the associative probabilistic-sum
t-conorm \citep{klement2004} sharing algebraic form with Leaky Noisy-OR
\citep{fallet2012}, and the conditional upper-bound step under independent
Bernoulli primitives applies the established Harris--FKG positive-association
principle also used in cascade-bounding literature \citep{abbe2017,abbe2020}.
These components are explicitly credited to prior literature and are not
claimed as newly invented operators or inequalities.
\item \textbf{Proponent-Designed Modular Adapter-to-Core Formulation and
Traceable Engine.} The proponents contribute a clean, domain-decoupled
single-pass DAG architecture that separates raw domain adapters (mapping
physical, advisory, or metering tables into bounded $[0,1]$ disturbance
$B_i$, susceptibility $S_{ij}$, and transmission $\tau_{ij}$) from a
fail-closed deterministic core engine and interactive inspection prototype.
Unlike unbounded additive pathway frameworks such as RIPA \citep{faraji2026}
or domain-coupled hydraulic and grid heuristics \citep{dastgir2023,zhang2025},
the design enforces explicit non-probabilistic index semantics, fail-closed
acyclicity and finiteness rejection, provenance tagging, and edge-level
contribution traceability.
\item \textbf{Algorithmic, Analytical-Evaluation, and Cross-Domain Empirical
Contributions.} Building strictly on the frozen core without modifying its
equations, the study contributes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Receiving-State Bracket Characterization:} an operational
characterization of the conditional bracket
$M_j \le T_j \le \pi_j \le R_j \le H_j$ for receiving-state prioritization
(distinct from seed-set spread maximization in
\citealp{kempe2003,chen2010mia,abbe2020}), quantifying slack, saturation, and
fixed-budget selection regret across more than 4,316 controlled scenarios;
\item \emph{Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening:} a
proponent-designed two-stage screening algorithm combining $O(|V|+|E|)$
Stage~1 $[M_j, R_j]$ certification with Stage~2 targeted $T_j$ refinement
restricted solely to the uncertified boundary band $U_k^{(1)}$, achieving
provably sound deterministic top-$k$ membership decisions with zero Monte
Carlo trials;
\item \emph{Self-Diagnosing Bracket-Width Reliability ($W_j = R_j - M_j$):}
an empirical evaluation of the zero-cost scalar width $W_j$ as an oracle-free
diagnostic for flagging high-distortion and reversal-prone regimes;
\item \emph{Controlled Shared-Ancestry Distortion Analysis:} exact paired
isolation of shared- vs.\ independent-origin distortion (560 matched branch
controls plus prospective connected-component ancestry diagnostics) mapping
both where scalar updates remain tight and where they fail; and
\item \emph{Cross-Domain Real-Data Instantiations and Reproducible Artifacts:}
provenance-verified Gate~A instantiations across urban drainage (the official
DPWH Cagayan de Oro Bitan-ag--Kolambog DAG from \texttt{FOI-REQ-2026-7383};
\citealp{dpwh2026foi,upplanades2016}), software dependencies
(\texttt{Express 4.18.2}), and electrical distribution
(\texttt{Caltech SoCal 28-Bus} Component~2; \citealp{xie2025}), delivered
alongside a frozen Git release, automated verification tests, SHA-256 output
manifests, and dual thesis/journal reporting pipelines.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}\label{scope-and-limitations}

\subsubsection{Scope of the Study}

The study covers the design, formal specification, implementation, and
evaluation of GBBRPM for static comparative node-risk assessment on DAGs. It
specifies bounded local disturbance, susceptibility, optional transmission,
edge contributions, node-state aggregation, topological evaluation, scalar
bracket-width diagnostics ($W_j = R_j - M_j$), Two-Stage Deterministic
Certified Top-$k$ Screening, and tie-aware ranking.

The restriction to directed acyclic graphs is a deliberate, principled design
boundary justified on three grounds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Finite Deterministic Termination without Cyclic
Double-Counting:} Acyclicity guarantees single-pass evaluation in topological
order with strict $O(|V|+|E|)$ time and memory complexity, terminating
finitely without fixed-point iteration, arbitrary damping factors, or
self-reinforcing cyclic amplification of a node's own disturbance.
\item \textbf{Structural Alignment with Targeted Infrastructure and Software
Subsystems:} Gravity-fed branched urban drainage reaches (such as the
verified DPWH Bitan-ag--Kolambog catchment DAG), acyclic software package
dependency trees (\texttt{Express 4.18.2}), and radial electrical
distribution feeders (\texttt{Caltech SoCal 28-Bus} Component~2) naturally
exhibit directed acyclic flow or dependency structure. Crucially, converging
junctions receiving two or more upstream branches (in-degree $\ge 2$, such as
the Agora confluence \texttt{J\_AGORA\_CONF} / PCSWMM \texttt{J5}) are valid
DAG structures rather than cycles.
\item \textbf{Validity of Topological Induction for the Receiving-State
Bracket:} The conditional Harris--FKG upper-bound proof and the full bracket
$M_j \le T_j \le \pi_j \le R_j \le H_j$ rely on topological induction and
acyclic independence between an upstream node's activation $X_i$ and its
outgoing edge transmission primitive $E_{ij}$; cycles break this inductive
structure.
\end{enumerate}

Controlled networks will support investigation of mathematical behavior,
mechanism effects, robustness, shared-origin reconvergence, certified
screening coverage, and computational cost. A separately specified stochastic
cascade benchmark will provide exact small-graph references and vectorized
Monte Carlo estimates with explicit Hoeffding sampling uncertainty
\citep{hoeffding1963}. Matched shared- and independent-origin branch
scenarios (560 controls) will form the primary controlled dependence
experiment; 3,600 uniform-weight generated DAGs, 144 heterogeneous-weight
DAGs, and 12 \texttt{Express 4.18.2} stochastic scenarios will support
receiving-state tightness, certified screening, and fixed-budget ranking
comparisons. These planned experiments are additional to the historical 136
exploratory runs. The analytic bracket and its proof are strictly conditional
on the benchmark assumptions; probabilistic score bounds are never asserted
for domain-index outputs.

Three real-data domain instantiations will be examined separately using
documented topology and parameter mappings: urban drainage via the official
DPWH FOI release (\texttt{FOI-REQ-2026-7383}, \citealp{dpwh2026foi,upplanades2016};
Gate~A pass for \texttt{drainage\_\allowbreak dpwh\_\allowbreak cdo\_\allowbreak bitanag\_\allowbreak kolambog\_\allowbreak v1} with 11
nodes, 11 edges, $S_{ij} \in [0.4639, 0.8471]$, real shared-ancestry
reconvergence at \texttt{J\_AGORA\_CONF}, and Tier-2 controlled blockage
injections, while Gate~B field flood hotspots remain exploratory context
only), software dependency (\texttt{Express 4.18.2}), and electrical
distribution (\texttt{Caltech SoCal 28-Bus} Component~2; \citealp{xie2025}).
The same core equations are retained across all three cases. The common
object of investigation is coherent instantiation of the architectural roles,
rather than equality of physical outcomes or comparability of numerical
scores across domains. Confirmatory operational validation (Gate~B) proceeds
only where aligned independent outcome records become available.

The research prototype will support validated synthetic and user-imported
DAG inputs, controlled parameter editing, execution of the versioned
evaluation engine, and traceable graph, contribution, and ranking outputs.
Functional verification will assess numerical consistency, fail-closed input
handling, and presentation of the computation.

\subsubsection{Limitations of the Study}

\textbf{Cycle Non-Applicability and Fail-Closed Policy.} The GBBRPM v1 core
is restricted strictly to finite DAGs and single-pass topological evaluation.
It does not represent directed cycles, bidirectional hydraulic or electrical
feedback, physical-time evolution, or full hydrodynamic or power-flow
dynamics. A topological order is a computational dependency order, not a
physical propagation timeline. Consistent with a fail-closed contract, any
supplied graph containing a directed cycle, self-loop, or bidirectional loop
is rejected with a reasoned validation error; the engine \emph{never}
performs silent edge deletion, heuristic cycle breaking, or hidden strongly
connected component (SCC) condensation. Consequently, looped urban stormwater
grids, surcharged backwater or reverse-flow regimes, meshed power networks,
and cyclic software dependency rings are explicitly out of scope for
GBBRPM~v1. Applying GBBRPM~v1 to an acyclic subsystem within a larger looped
network requires an explicit, external, documented acyclic sub-catchment or
radial-feeder extraction with every excluded edge and interpretation boundary
logged, whereas native feedback modeling requires a separately formulated
iterative or SCC-based extension in future work.

\textbf{Direct-Neighbor (Local-State) Assumption and Shared-Ancestry
Collapse.} By architectural design, the scalar GBBRPM core obeys a
\emph{direct-neighbor (local-state) assumption}: computing the receiving
state $R_j$ at node $j$ reads only the scalar states $R_i$ of its immediate
predecessors $i \in P(j)$ without retaining upstream path history or
origin-lineage identifiers. While this local-state restriction enables
$O(|V|+|E|)$ evaluation, it is the direct structural cause of shared-ancestry
collapse: whenever disturbance from an uncertain common upstream origin
splits across parallel paths and reconverges at a downstream junction (such
as \texttt{J\_AGORA\_CONF} in the Bitan-ag--Kolambog drainage reach or
synthetic diamond graphs), the scalar complement-product rule aggregates
incoming contributions as though they arose from separate origins. Under the
stochastic reference, this produces one-sided overestimation ($R_j \ge \pi_j$)
and can induce pairwise rank reversals if upper slack is unequal across
candidates. The study does not conceal this limitation; instead, it
quantifies the distortion explicitly through matched branch controls, the
scalar width $W_j = R_j - M_j$, and the separate ancestry-aware diagnostic in
Chapter~3 (which itself may discard legitimate cumulative effects and is not
presumed superior).

\textbf{Semantic, Identifiability, and Domain-Evidence Boundaries.} The
GBBRPM index is not a calibrated probability, and susceptibility $S_{ij}$ and
transmission $\tau_{ij}$ cannot be identified separately from output states
alone because outputs depend on their product $w_{ij} = S_{ij}\tau_{ij}$. The
stochastic benchmark quantifies agreement only with its declared
independent-primitive process; it does not establish that advisory severity,
hydraulic loading ratios, or electrical loading ratios are calibrated event
probabilities. Interpretation depends on the provenance, coverage, and
defensibility of domain inputs. Synthetic networks, Tier-2 controlled
blockage injections on the DPWH Bitan-ag--Kolambog reach, and simulated SWMM
hydraulic outputs are not field failure observations, and the June~10, 2025
DPWH flood-hotspot records remain exploratory context rather than Gate~B
confirmatory ground truth because they reflect existing-channel conditions
rather than the 2016 proposed Master Plan diversion topology and lack
non-flooded controls and pre-event blockage measurements. Likewise,
advisory-based software mappings do not establish exploitation probabilities,
and controlled disturbances applied to measured electrical loading do not
establish event-response prediction because the available baseline is not
aligned with independent switching-window outcomes.

Higher-fidelity formulations may be required for cyclic feedback, correlated
external shocks, shared dependencies, and physical state interactions
\citep{buldyrev2010,shuang2017,petrone2018,wu2026,dastgir2024,zhang2024}.
The study reports these omissions as explicit accuracy--cost tradeoffs rather
than presuming that reduced computational cost preserves predictive fidelity.
Prototype checks establish functional consistency in the tested
configuration, not production security, multi-user readiness, usability, or
operational utility.

\subsection{Conceptual Framework}\label{conceptual-framework}

The conceptual framework of the study was designed by the proponents to
separate domain-specific input construction from a reusable, bounded,
single-pass propagation core and a rigorous comparative evaluation harness.
At the mathematical level, the framework begins with local disturbance and
directed acyclic network context. The current risk state at an upstream node
is transformed into a relation-specific contribution according to
susceptibility and, where justified, optional transmission. Multiple incoming
contributions are then combined with any local disturbance at the receiving
node using the established complement-product aggregation rule
\citep{klement2004}, producing an updated comparative risk state. That
updated state can in turn influence later downstream relations, allowing the
modeled risk state to evolve recursively through the DAG. The resulting node
states provide the basis for comparative prioritization.

Figure~\ref{fig:conceptual-framework} illustrates the equation-level data
flow within the propagation core, distinguishing domain-supplied inputs, the
generic bounded propagation core, and the comparative decision-support output.

\input{figures/fig_conceptual_framework}

For a directed relation $i\to j$, the generic edge contribution is:

\[Q_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}R_i\eqtag{1-1}\]

where $R_i$ is the current upstream normalized risk state, $S_{ij}$ is
relation-specific susceptibility, and $\tau_{ij}$ is optional transmission or
influence.

The receiving-node state is:

\[R_j=1-(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij})\eqtag{1-2}\]

where $B_j$ is the local disturbance and $P(j)$ is the set of predecessors of
node $j$. This is the established probabilistic-sum t-conorm extended
to the local and incoming inputs \citep{klement2004}. Its adoption supplies
bounded saturating aggregation, not a newly invented operator. Despite
the related noisy-OR algebra \citep{fallet2012}, this index is not
interpreted as a probabilistic union because its inputs are not calibrated
probabilities.

The resulting $R_j$ is the node's current modeled receiving state, including
local and inherited disturbance. It is not an estimate of the benefit from
mitigating that node. It serves as the upstream state for later relations,
so subsequent calculations retain accumulated influence. Node ranking is
derived post-pass from the final receiving states rather than treated as an
additional propagation mechanism.

Embedding this mathematical core inside a complete research system,
Figure~\ref{fig:unified_architecture} presents the unified end-to-end GBBRPM
architecture and evaluation framework designed by the proponents. Layer~1
decouples domain-specific data adapters (the DPWH Bitan-ag--Kolambog drainage
reach, \texttt{Express 4.18.2}, and \texttt{Caltech SoCal 28-Bus}
Component~2) and synthetic benchmark generators so that domain semantics are
never conflated across cases. Layer~2 enforces fail-closed input and
acyclicity validation before executing the $O(|V|+|E|)$ topological DAG
propagator, storing full $(B_j, Q_{ij}, R_j)$ traces, applying tie-aware
ranking, and computing the zero-cost scalar bracket width $W_j = R_j - M_j$.
Layer~3 integrates matched comparators, exact and bit-packed Monte Carlo
stochastic reference solvers, and the proponent-designed Two-Stage
Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening harness built directly on
$M_j \le T_j \le \pi_j \le R_j$. Layer~4 emits traceable quantitative metrics,
powers the interactive React/Vite and FastAPI research prototype from the
exact same versioned core, and generates SHA-256-verified dual thesis and
journal artifacts. A separately specified stochastic reference supports
controlled evaluation of the recursion; domain usefulness requires
independent outcomes rather than agreement with the index itself.

Completed exploratory calculations or tests used to assess feasibility and limitations before protocol freezing, distinguished from independent confirmatory or outcome validation. \\

Normalized risk state. &
A dimensionless continuous value in $[0,1]$ representing the current modeled level of network-context risk at a node. It is not a calibrated probability of an event. \\

Susceptibility. &

Edge contribution. &

Aggregation through the established probabilistic-sum t-conorm, combining local disturbance and incoming contributions while preserving the $[0,1]$ index range. \\

Domain-specific instantiation. &
Defining the shared node, edge, disturbance, susceptibility, and transmission roles with defensible meanings and inputs for a particular domain. \\

Generic formulation. &
A core formulation not hard-coded to one domain. Reusability of its architecture does not establish universal empirical applicability. \\
