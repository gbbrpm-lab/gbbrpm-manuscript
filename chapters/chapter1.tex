\section{Introduction}
\hyphenation{GBBRPM}

\subsection{Background of the Study}\label{background-of-the-study}

Network propagation models represent connected entities and the relations
through which one entity can influence another. Across applications, the
propagated quantity may be activation, compromise, disturbance, or risk,
and its meaning determines the appropriate update rule
\citep{kempe2003,munoz2017,faraji2026}. A local observation and a receiving
node's accumulated state are consequently different analytical quantities.
When a model includes inherited influence, nodes with similar local inputs
can acquire different receiving states because their predecessors and
relation conditions differ. Comparative assessment therefore requires an
explicit account of both the local state and the permitted network influence.

Several modeling choices shape this account. Graph topology determines
which influences can reach a node; edge modifiers determine how strongly
upstream state contributes; recursion determines whether inherited state
continues along later relations; and aggregation determines how local and
incoming terms combine. These choices can change magnitudes and rankings.
Structural position, accumulated receiving state, influence exerted by a
source, and benefit from intervention are distinct targets. An evaluation
must identify its target before treating a high score as useful priority.

The mathematical components relevant to bounded propagation already have
established precedents. The probabilistic-sum t-conorm combines bounded
inputs through complement product \citep{klement2004}. Leaky noisy-OR uses
related background-plus-parent algebra in a probabilistic setting
\citep{fallet2012}, while RIPA propagates current risk through relation and
receiver modifiers with additive aggregation \citep{faraji2026}. MIA restricts
cascade calculations to local influence trees, and association-based cascade
bounds provide another precedent for analyzing approximation
\citep{chen2010mia,abbe2017}. The research value of selecting these mechanisms
will therefore depend on a justified formulation and comparative evidence.
Boundedness alone establishes neither correct receiving-state rankings nor
practical usefulness.

Shared ancestry provides a specific condition for investigating these
choices. Incoming contributions may originate from different local sources
or from one origin whose influence splits and reconverges. Separate
aggregation of those terms can produce different results depending on the
assumed process and dependence structure. Restricted-tree methods and
cascade bounds explicitly address related approximation questions
\citep{chen2010mia,abbe2017}. The present study will examine how source
uncertainty, attenuation, and graph structure affect the selected recursion's
reference agreement and priority ordering, including regimes where simpler
rules or dependence treatments perform equally well or better.

\begin{samepage}
The study will specify and evaluate the \textbf{Graph-Based Bounded Risk
Propagation Model (GBBRPM)}, the name assigned here to a particular
architecture within graph-based risk propagation. Its core will combine local
disturbance with modified current upstream states using the established
complement-product operator on \textbf{directed acyclic graphs (DAGs)}.
In domain applications, its output will be a dimensionless receiving-state
index in $[0,1]$. The core equations will be shared, while the meanings and
construction of local disturbance, susceptibility, and transmission will
remain specific to each domain. A \textbf{GBBRPM evaluation engine} will
implement the versioned equations and expose their input and contribution
traces through a research prototype.\par
\end{samepage}

The primary controlled evaluation will use a separately defined stochastic
activation process with independent initial and conditional edge events.
This construction will supply exact or uncertainty-qualified reference
activation probabilities for matched comparisons. Under those assumptions,
the GBBRPM recursion has a conditional nodewise upper-bound interpretation;
Chapter 3 will state its bracket, proof, and relation to established bounding
arguments. Experiments will evaluate bound tightness, fixed-budget receiving-
node selection, and computational cost. This benchmark will characterize the
mechanism under its specified process. Domain-index inputs will retain their
own meanings, and practical usefulness will require independent outcomes
appropriate to the selected domain task.

Urban drainage supplied the original practical motivation. Camera-based
blockage assessment provides local condition information, while graph-based
critical-pipe and weakest-link analyses evaluate connected-system behavior
\citep{vandaele2024,dastgir2023,meijer2023}. These approaches illustrate why
local condition and network-context assessment deserve separate attention.
The proposal will retain drainage alongside software-dependency and electrical-
network cases as supporting instantiations of the common core. Their purpose
will be to examine defensible mappings, DAG eligibility, and evidence limits.
Three instantiations will not establish universal applicability, and their
scores will not be pooled as a common physical risk scale. Preliminary
worked calculations and exploratory findings will be retained in the
appendices; the proposed evaluation protocols will be specified in Chapter 3.

\subsection{Statement of the Problem}\label{statement-of-the-problem}

The central problem is to determine when GBBRPM's complement-product
recursion gives useful receiving-state rankings under a declared reference
process, how shared ancestry limits that agreement, and what computational
tradeoffs accompany alternative mechanisms. Existing aggregation,
restricted-tree approximation, and cascade-bounding methods establish
relevant precedents \citep{klement2004,chen2010mia,abbe2017}. The proposed
study will therefore assess the specified architecture against matched
comparators rather than infer its contribution from a new name, a bounded
formula, or different scores alone.

The controlled comparison will hold graph and primitive inputs fixed across
methods while varying predeclared source, attenuation, and topology conditions.
Maximum and clipped-additive aggregation, local-only scoring, a recursion
ablation, a restricted-tree comparator, and the ancestry-aware diagnostic will
provide distinct comparison conditions. The analytic benchmark bracket will
establish nodewise score bounds under independent primitive events; the
experiments will determine their tightness and the resulting selection quality.
A tighter score bound will not automatically imply a better ordering.

This evaluation will address a receiving-state task: accumulated local and
inherited influence at candidate nodes. Its stochastic reference will define
activation probabilities only within the constructed process. Domain indices
will not be relabeled as event probabilities, and a high receiving-state
score will not be interpreted as source influence or intervention benefit.
Drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network cases will assess
coherent instantiation separately; any practical prioritization claim will
require independent evidence relevant to that case's task.

The intended lightweight design will be assessed through matched runtime and
memory measurements, required inputs, and preprocessing costs. A single-pass
DAG core can have limited computational requirements while still omitting
important dependence or physical mechanisms. The study will investigate
that accuracy--cost tradeoff and report unfavorable or unresolved findings.
It will address the following research questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item How can local disturbance, current upstream state, relation-specific
modifiers, and bounded aggregation be formulated into GBBRPM for
comparative node-risk prioritization on DAGs?
\item How can the proposed formulation be implemented reproducibly in a
GBBRPM evaluation engine and presented in a prototype that preserves
input validation and computational traceability?
\item Under an explicitly specified stochastic cascade reference, how do
GBBRPM, maximum aggregation, clipped additive aggregation, local-only
scoring, a local-upstream recursion ablation, and a restricted-tree
comparator differ in bound tightness and fixed-budget node selection?
\item How do shared ancestry, source uncertainty, and attenuation affect
the receiving-state bracket's tightness and ranking quality; when does an ancestry-aware diagnostic
improve or worsen them; and what mathematical and robustness properties
characterize the frozen core?
\item What runtime, memory, and input requirements accompany GBBRPM,
the matched comparators, and the reference solvers at stated precision?
\item Can the same core be instantiated coherently in drainage,
software-dependency, and electrical-network cases, and what applicability
limits follow from each case's data and evaluation?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Objectives of the Study}\label{objectives-of-the-study}

\subsubsection{General Objective}\label{general-objective}

The general objective is to specify, implement, and evaluate GBBRPM for
bounded receiving-state prioritization on directed acyclic graphs, determine
its reference accuracy and computational tradeoffs under controlled
aggregation and dependence conditions, and establish the evidence limits
of its drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network instantiations.

\subsubsection{Specific Objectives}\label{specific-objectives}

Specifically, the study aims to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item formulate GBBRPM by specifying its DAG representation, bounded state
semantics, local disturbance, relation-specific modifiers, recursive update
rule, aggregation mechanism, assumptions, and expected mathematical
properties;
\item implement a reproducible deterministic evaluation engine and an
interactive research prototype that will validate inputs, execute the same
versioned core, and expose traceable node states, edge contributions, and
rankings;
\item evaluate receiving-state error and fixed-budget node selection against
exact or uncertainty-qualified stochastic references using matched aggregation
comparators, a recursion ablation, and a restricted-tree comparator,
while separately characterizing
heterogeneous-input effects and saturation;
\item characterize shared-origin dependence under controlled source and
attenuation changes, evaluate an ancestry-aware diagnostic against the same
reference, derive the benchmark bracket with its assumptions, and establish
the frozen core's analytical properties, boundary
behavior, sensitivity, and perturbation robustness;
\item characterize the evaluation engine's computational and input
requirements through complexity analysis, runtime and memory measurements,
and a documented comparison of required information and modeled mechanisms;
and
\item assess whether the same core formulation can be instantiated
coherently in the three selected domains through documented DAG
construction, parameter mappings, within-domain experiments, and explicit
evidence and applicability limits.
\end{enumerate}

Literature synthesis will inform the design requirements and interpretation
of these objectives. The principal research outputs will be the specified
architecture, comparative evaluation evidence, and an account of accuracy,
cost, and applicability conditions. The evaluation engine and prototype
will support reproducibility and inspection of those outputs.

\subsection{Significance of the Study}\label{significance-of-the-study}

\subsubsection{Theoretical Significance}

The study will specify an architecture for combining
local disturbance with recursively propagated network context on DAGs.
The proposed methodological significance will lie in a reference-based
account of when the adopted recursion gives accurate receiving-state estimates
under stated stochastic assumptions, how shared origins affect that agreement,
and what computational tradeoffs accompany alternative rules. Basic operator
properties and the architecture's name will not stand as the original result.
The declared index semantics will remain separate from benchmark probabilities.
Under the synthetic independent-primitive process, the same GBBRPM recursion
will be a nodewise upper bound; that conditional mathematical interpretation
will not calibrate domain inputs. Existing association-based cascade bounds
will be compared before any novelty claim \citep{abbe2017}.
The study will distinguish properties established by the formulation from
claims that require computational or domain evidence, including the
difference between bounded aggregation and correction for shared-origin
dependence.

Existing propagation and risk literature supplies relevant mechanisms
\citep{kempe2003,buldyrev2010,sheyner2002,munoz2017,petrone2018}. The proposed
contribution will be assessed through the specification and evaluation
of the selected comparative-state task. The adopted t-conorm and its
basic properties will be credited as established mathematics
\citep{klement2004}; their use or rederivation will not be claimed as
a new aggregation theory. A common interface for domain-specific inputs will allow architectural
reuse to be examined without assuming common physical semantics or
universal applicability. Susceptibility and transmission will retain
distinct conceptual roles, while their separate empirical identification
will not be inferred from outputs that depend on their product.

\subsubsection{Practical Significance}

The evaluation engine and prototype will provide an inspectable workflow for
examining local inputs, inherited contributions, and resulting receiving-state
rankings. The comparison will characterize how the simple scalar recursion and
alternative mechanisms differ in benchmark agreement and selected priorities. Runtime, memory, and
input requirements will make the cost of those choices explicit.

The drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network cases will document
how the common architecture can be instantiated and where the available
information restricts its interpretation. Potential screening or inspection
uses will depend on defensible parameter construction and independent
outcome evidence within each domain. Lower cost, plausible ordering, and
coherent mappings will not alone establish maintenance benefit, exploitation
prediction, or fault prediction. Operational decision claims will be limited
to the tasks and outcomes actually evaluated.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}\label{scope-and-limitations}

\subsubsection{Scope of the Study}

The study will cover the formulation and implementation of GBBRPM for
static comparative node-risk assessment on DAGs. It will specify bounded
local disturbance, susceptibility, optional transmission, edge
contributions, node-state aggregation, topological evaluation, and
tie-aware ranking. Controlled networks will support investigation of
mathematical behavior, mechanism effects, robustness, shared-origin
reconvergence, and computational cost. A separately specified stochastic
cascade benchmark will provide exact small-graph references and Monte Carlo
estimates with explicit sampling uncertainty. Matched shared- and independent-
origin scenarios will be the main controlled dependence experiment; larger
DAGs will support receiving-state and fixed-budget ranking comparisons.
These planned experiments will be additional to the historical 136 runs.
The bound and its proof will be conditional on the benchmark assumptions;
probabilistic score bounds will not be asserted for domain-index outputs.

Three domain instantiations will be examined separately using documented
topology and parameter mappings: drainage, software dependency, and
electrical networks. The same core equations will be retained. The common
object of investigation will be coherent instantiation of the architectural
roles, rather than equality of physical outcomes or comparability of
numerical scores across domains. Operational drainage or event-aligned
electrical evaluation will proceed only if suitable data become available.

The research prototype will support validated synthetic and user-imported
DAG inputs, controlled parameter editing, execution of the versioned
evaluation engine, and traceable graph and ranking outputs. Functional
verification will assess numerical consistency, input handling, and
presentation of the computation.

\subsubsection{Limitations of the Study}

The core will be restricted to DAGs and one-pass evaluation. It will not
represent cycles, bidirectional feedback, physical-time evolution, or full
hydraulic or electrical dynamics. A topological order is a computational
dependency order, not a physical propagation timeline. The index will not
be a calibrated probability, and the local aggregator will not correct
shared-origin dependence. Susceptibility and transmission will not be identifiable separately 
from output states alone. The stochastic benchmark will
quantify agreement only with its declared independent primitive process.
It will not establish that advisory severity, hydraulic loading, or electrical
conditions are calibrated event probabilities. The ancestry-aware diagnostic
may discard legitimate cumulative effects and will not be presumed superior.

Interpretation will depend on the provenance, coverage, and defensibility
of domain inputs. Synthetic networks and simulated hydraulic outputs will
not be treated as field observations. Advisory-based software mappings
will not establish exploitation probabilities. Controlled disturbances
applied to measured electrical loading will not establish event-response
prediction. The currently available electrical baseline is not aligned
with independent outcomes for the documented switching windows.
Inadequate field records or event-aligned outcomes may restrict evaluation
to structural instantiation and controlled behavior.

Higher-fidelity formulations may be required for feedback, shared
dependencies, and physical state interactions
\citep{buldyrev2010,shuang2017,petrone2018,wu2026,dastgir2024,zhang2024}.
The study will report these omissions as tradeoffs rather than presume
that reduced computational cost preserves predictive fidelity. Prototype
checks will establish consistency in the tested configuration, not
production security, multi-user readiness, usability, or operational
utility.

\subsection{Conceptual Framework}\label{conceptual-framework}

The conceptual framework of the study begins with local disturbance and
directed acyclic network context. The current risk state at an upstream node is
transformed into a relation-specific contribution according to
susceptibility and, where justified, optional transmission. Multiple
incoming contributions are then combined with any local disturbance at
the receiving node using a bounded aggregation rule, producing an
updated comparative risk state. That updated state can in turn influence
later downstream relations, allowing the modeled risk state to evolve
recursively through the network. The resulting node states provide the
basis for comparative prioritization.

Figure~\ref{fig:conceptual-framework} distinguishes the domain-supplied
inputs, the generic bounded propagation core, and the comparative
decision-support output.

\input{figures/fig_conceptual_framework}

For a directed relation $i\to j$, the generic edge contribution is:

\[Q_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}R_i\eqtag{1-1}\]

where $R_i$ is the current upstream normalized risk state, $S_{ij}$ is
relation-specific susceptibility, and $\tau_{ij}$ is optional transmission or
influence.

The receiving-node state is:

\[R_j=1-(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij})\eqtag{1-2}\]

where $B_j$ is the local disturbance and $P(j)$ is the set of predecessors of
node $j$. This is the established probabilistic-sum t-conorm extended
to the local and incoming inputs \citep{klement2004}. Its adoption supplies
bounded saturating aggregation, not a newly invented operator. Despite
the related noisy-OR algebra, this index is not interpreted as a
probabilistic union because its inputs are not calibrated probabilities.

The resulting $R_j$ is the node's current modeled receiving state, including
local and inherited disturbance. It is not an estimate of the benefit from
mitigating that node. It can serve as the upstream state for later relations,
so subsequent calculations retain the accumulated influence. Node ranking
will be derived from the final receiving states rather than treated as an
additional propagation mechanism.

A separately specified stochastic reference will support
controlled evaluation of the recursion; domain usefulness will require
independent outcomes rather than agreement with the index itself.

Comparative risk prioritization. &
Ranking components according to their modeled risk states relative to one another under the same network and parameterization. \\
\addlinespace
 
GBBRPM. &
Graph-Based Bounded Risk Propagation Model, the name assigned in this study to its proposed bounded, non-probabilistic comparative-risk model on DAGs; not an established model-family name. \\
\addlinespace
 
GBBRPM evaluation engine. &
Software that will validate inputs and apply the versioned GBBRPM equations in topological order; the implementation of the model. \\
\addlinespace

Directed acyclic graph (DAG). &
A directed graph containing no directed cycle. This is the supported graph class for the proposed one-pass engine. \\
\addlinespace

Lightweight model. &
A fixed-form, single-pass DAG model without physical-time integration or training in its core. Runtime, memory, preprocessing, and input requirements will characterize this design; reduced cost does not establish physical fidelity. \\
\addlinespace

Stochastic reference. &
A separately defined activation process with independent initial and conditional
edge events, used to evaluate the recursion under declared probabilistic assumptions;
not a calibration of the domain risk index. \\
\addlinespace

Shared ancestry. &
Overlap between the local-origin lineages reaching different incoming relations.
Structural overlap alone does not specify the magnitude of event dependence. \\
\addlinespace

Receiving-state ranking. &
Ordering nodes by accumulated local and inherited modeled disturbance;
distinct from ranking the benefit of intervention at those nodes. \\
\addlinespace

Preliminary evidence. &
Completed exploratory calculations or tests used to assess feasibility and limitations before protocol freezing, distinguished from independent confirmatory or outcome validation. \\
\addlinespace

Normalized risk state. &
A dimensionless continuous value in $[0,1]$ representing the current modeled level of network-context risk at a node. It is not a calibrated probability of an event. \\
\addlinespace
 
Local disturbance. &
A bounded input representing risk or disturbance introduced directly at a node according to the selected domain. \\
\addlinespace
 
Susceptibility. &
A bounded relation-specific factor representing how strongly a relation or receiving context responds under the evaluated condition. \\
\addlinespace
 
Transmission or influence. &
An optional bounded factor describing how strongly influence transfers along an existing directed relation. \\
\addlinespace
 
Edge contribution. &
The relation-specific contribution generated from current upstream risk, susceptibility, and optional transmission. \\
\addlinespace
 
Topology or reachability. &
The directed network structure that determines which nodes and relations can participate in propagation. \\
\addlinespace
 
Bounded aggregation. &
Aggregation through the established probabilistic-sum t-conorm, combining local disturbance and incoming contributions while preserving the $[0,1]$ index range. \\
\addlinespace
 
Domain-specific instantiation. &
Defining the shared node, edge, disturbance, susceptibility, and transmission roles with defensible meanings and inputs for a particular domain. \\
\addlinespace
 
Generic formulation. &
A core formulation not hard-coded to one domain. Reusability of its architecture does not establish universal empirical applicability. \\
