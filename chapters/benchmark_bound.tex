\subsubsection{Benchmark Bracket and Its Interpretation}
\label{benchmark-bracket}

To make the analytical scope of the stochastic benchmark explicit,
Table~\ref{tab:bracket-assumptions} formalizes the five assumptions
(A1--A5) required by the receiving-state bracket proposition below.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Formal assumptions (A1--A5) underlying the receiving-state bracket proposition.}
\label{tab:bracket-assumptions}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.5cm}L@{}}

\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Assumption} & \textbf{Formal Requirement and Enforcement Contract}\\
\midrule
\textbf{A1} & \textbf{Finite DAG} &
$G=(V,E)$ is finite and directed acyclic; cycles, self-loops, and duplicate
directed edges trigger fail-closed rejection rather than silent modification.\\
\addlinespace[4pt]
\textbf{A2} & \textbf{Strictly Finite Bounded Inputs} &
All supplied or derived $B_j,S_{ij},\tau_{ij}\in[0,1]$ (hence

$\pm\infty$ inputs are rejected fail-closed prior to clipping or propagation.\\
\addlinespace[4pt]
\textbf{A3} & \textbf{Independent Bernoulli Primitives} &
In the stochastic reference (Equation~(3-10)), all local activations
$Z_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(B_j)$ and edge transmissions
$E_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(w_{ij})$ are mutually independent.\\
\addlinespace[4pt]
\textbf{A4} & \textbf{Monotone Boolean Recursion (Harris--FKG)} &
Each receiving-node activation $X_j$ is an increasing Boolean function of
the primitive Bernoulli variables, ensuring positive association of incoming
failure events $F_{ij}$ under the product Bernoulli measure via Harris--FKG.\\
\addlinespace[4pt]
\textbf{A5} & \textbf{Unthresholded Path-Preserving In-Arborescence} &
For each target $j$, $T_j$ is evaluated on a consistent maximum-weight

ancestor without arbitrary path-weight pruning.\\

Violating any assumption in Table~\ref{tab:bracket-assumptions} produces a
specific failure mode rather than a benign approximation shift. Breaking
\textbf{A1} through directed cycles or hydraulic backwater loops eliminates
topological order, causing non-termination or cyclic double-counting in a
single-pass recursion. Breaking \textbf{A2} through non-finite values
(\texttt{NaN} or $\pm\infty$) corrupts load-to-capacity clipping and invalidates
$[0,1]$ boundedness. Breaking \textbf{A3} or \textbf{A4}---for example through
correlated external shocks, common-cause edge failures, or non-monotone
inhibitory interactions---breaks product-measure positive association and
invalidates the Harris--FKG upper bound $\pi_j\le R_j$. Breaking \textbf{A5}
through arbitrary path-weight thresholding or inconsistent edge deletion can
discard a maximizing ancestor path and violate $M_j\le T_j\le\pi_j$. Every
executable contract and analytical guarantee will be verified by an automated
\texttt{pytest} regression suite enforcing fail-closed \texttt{ValueError}
rejection on malformed inputs, exact enumeration parity against
Equation~(3-15) to absolute error $<10^{-12}$ with exact configuration
probability mass equal to $1.0$, zero bracket violations across all 560
matched-branch and 720 development-DAG scenarios, and zero false
certifications in the top-$k$ screening harness below.

\textit{Proposition (receiving-state bracket under independent primitives).}
Under Assumptions~A1--A5 (Table~\ref{tab:bracket-assumptions}), let
$G=(V,E)$ be a finite DAG and let all initial-activation and
edge-transmission variables in Equation~(3-10) be mutually independent, with
strictly finite supplied parameters in $[0,1]$. On the same graph and inputs,
define recursive maximum and clipped-additive states by
\[
M_j=\max\left(B_j,\max_{i\in P(j)}w_{ij}M_i\right),\qquad
H_j=\min\left(1,B_j+\sum_{i\in P(j)}w_{ij}H_i\right),
\eqtag{3-12}
\]
where the incoming maximum is zero for an empty predecessor set. Let $R_j$
be ordinary GBBRPM and $\pi_j=\Pr(X_j=1)$ under the benchmark. Let $T_j$
be the exact activation probability on the target-specific restricted
in-arborescence defined in Section~\ref{reference-comparators}. It will
retain a consistent maximum-weight path from every positive-path ancestor,
the original local inputs and weights, and target $j$ itself; no path-weight
threshold will be applied. Then
\[
M_j\le T_j\le\pi_j\le R_j\le H_j\qquad\text{for every }j\in V.
\eqtag{3-13}
\]

\textit{Proof.} In a DAG, $X_i$ cannot depend on the primitive $E_{ij}$,
and no incoming activation at $j$ can depend on $Z_j$. Independence of
the primitives therefore gives
$\Pr(X_i\land E_{ij})=w_{ij}\pi_i$. Since activation at $j$ includes
its own initial event and every successful incoming event,
$\pi_j\ge\max(B_j,\max_i w_{ij}\pi_i)$. A predecessor-free node has
$M_j=\pi_j=B_j$; induction over topological order establishes the lower bound.

The maximum recursion also has the path representation
$M_j=\max_{a,\gamma:a\rightarrow j}B_a\prod_{e\in\gamma}w_e$,
including the zero-length path at $j$. The restricted tree retains a
maximum-weight path for each positive-path ancestor by consistent next-edge
choices. Activation at $a$ through its own local event followed by successful
transmission along that retained path is sufficient to activate $j$ in the
tree. Its probability is $B_a\prod_{e\in\gamma}w_e$ by primitive independence.
The tree probability is at least each such path contribution and $B_j$, so
$M_j\le T_j$. Ancestors without a positive path contribute zero to $M_j$.
Couple the restricted and full processes using the same local and edge draws.
Deleting edges cannot create an activation, hence $T_j\le\pi_j$.
On the target-directed tree, incoming ancestor subtrees have disjoint
primitives; complement-product recursion therefore evaluates $T_j$ exactly.
This argument relies on the stated unthresholded, path-preserving construction;
arbitrary pruning need not retain the $T_j\ge M_j$ guarantee.

For the upper bound, let $F_{ij}$ denote failure of incoming activation
through $(i,j)$, that is, the complement of $\{X_i=1,E_{ij}=1\}$.
Every $X_i$ is an increasing Boolean function of the primitive variables,
so every $F_{ij}$ is decreasing. Positive association under a product
Bernoulli measure, the Harris/FKG principle, gives
$\Pr(\bigcap_iF_{ij})\ge\prod_i\Pr(F_{ij})$. This established principle
is also used for cascade upper bounds by \citet{abbe2017,abbe2020}; it is not a
new correlation inequality. Since $Z_j$ is independent of the incoming
events,
\[
\pi_j=1-(1-B_j)\Pr\left(\bigcap_{i\in P(j)}F_{ij}\right)
\le1-(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-w_{ij}\pi_i).
\eqtag{3-14}
\]
Complement product is increasing in each predecessor marginal.
Starting from $\pi_j=R_j=B_j$ at predecessor-free nodes, topological
induction yields $\pi_j\le R_j$.

Finally, for nonnegative inputs in $[0,1]$,
$1-\prod_k(1-x_k)\le\min(1,\sum_kx_k)$. Applying this inequality to
the local term and incoming contributions, followed by topological
induction and monotonicity of clipped addition, gives $R_j\le H_j$.
Primitive probabilities of zero or one follow directly or by continuity.
This completes the argument.

The proposition will be presented as a specialization of established
association and bounded-aggregation arguments, not as a verified first
theorem about cascade approximation. Abbe et al.'s journal treatment
also bounds individual activation probabilities before summing them into
seed-set influence. Its upper method uses length-indexed messages; its
DAG-based lower method uses a different incoming-message recurrence
\citep{abbe2020}. Section~\ref{cascade-bounds-prior-art} compares the
constructions and limits. Independent local activation
can also be represented by one certainly active auxiliary source with
independent edges of probabilities $B_j$ to all original nodes. That
construction facilitates comparison with fixed-seed literature; it will
not conceal the model's mathematical precedents.

For this benchmark, score discrepancy is one-sided: $R_j-\pi_j\ge0$.
Its scalar certificate is $0\le R_j-\pi_j\le R_j-M_j$.
When the tree comparator is computed, the tighter certificate is
$0\le R_j-\pi_j\le R_j-T_j\le R_j-M_j$.
Computing $T_j$ requires tree construction and evaluation for each requested
target; the tighter certificate is not free relative to the scalar pass.
Its reporting will reuse the already computed comparator result.
GBBRPM is no looser than recursive clipped addition as a nodewise upper
bound; this ordering need not be rediscovered experimentally. Experiments
will measure bound width, actual slack, conditions of equality, and
ranking or selection effects. A smaller pointwise score error does not
guarantee better rank order. The condition $T_a>R_b$ certifies
$\pi_a>\pi_b$ when tree bounds are available; $M_a>R_b$ remains a cheaper
sufficient certificate. Overlapping brackets remain inconclusive,
not evidence of a reversed ordering.

These guarantees concern only the stated independent-primitive benchmark.
They do not certify probabilities or physical bounds for the domain index,
dependent initial events, correlated transmission primitives, feedback, or
other cascade mechanisms. The ancestry-aware diagnostic will require its
own analysis and will not inherit the GBBRPM upper-bound guarantee.

\subsubsection{Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-\texorpdfstring{$k$}{k} Screening and Self-Diagnosing Reliability}
\label{certified-screening}

Built strictly on the established receiving-state bracket
$M_j\le T_j\le\pi_j\le R_j\le H_j$ (Equation~(3-13)) with zero changes to
the core GBBRPM update equations (Equations~(3-1)--(3-5)), the study
specifies a two-stage deterministic screening procedure for fixed-budget
receiving-state top-$k$ prioritization over the candidate set
$C=\{j\in V:B_j=0\}$ defined in Section~\ref{reference-metrics}. For any
nontrivial budget $1\le k<|C|$ (when $k=|C|$, all candidates are trivially
included), let $R_{(1)}\ge R_{(2)}\ge\dots\ge R_{(|C|)}$ and
$M_{(1)}\ge M_{(2)}\ge\dots\ge M_{(|C|)}$ denote the descending order
statistics of $R_j$ and $M_j$ over $C$.

\textbf{Stage 1 ($O(|V|+|E|)$ global scalar pass).}
A single topological pass evaluates both $M_j$ (Equation~(3-12)) and $R_j$
(Equation~(3-4)) for every node, yielding nodewise brackets $[M_j,R_j]$ over
$C$. Using the $(k+1)$-th largest upper bound $R_{(k+1)}$ and the $k$-th
largest lower bound $M_{(k)}$, Stage~1 partitions $C$ into a certified-IN
set, a certified-OUT set, and an uncertified boundary band:
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_k^{(1)}&=\{a\in C : M_a > R_{(k+1)}\},\\
O_k^{(1)}&=\{b\in C : R_b < M_{(k)}\},\\
U_k^{(1)}&=C\setminus\bigl(I_k^{(1)}\cup O_k^{(1)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\eqtag[Stage 1 scalar certified screening partition]{3-14a}
\]
Every node in $I_k^{(1)}\cup O_k^{(1)}$ is resolved deterministically with
zero Monte Carlo trials and zero per-target tree constructions. If
$U_k^{(1)}=\emptyset$, Stage~1 fully certifies the exact top-$k$ set
$\mathcal{T}_k^*=I_k^{(1)}$ in linear propagation time plus candidate
sorting.

\textbf{Stage 2 (targeted $T_j$ refinement on $U_k^{(1)}$ only).}
When $U_k^{(1)}\ne\emptyset$, the procedure computes the target-specific
restricted in-arborescence lower bound $T_j$
(Section~\ref{reference-comparators}) strictly for boundary nodes
$j\in U_k^{(1)}$, reducing per-target tree work from
$O(|V|(|V|+|E|))$ to $O(|U_k^{(1)}|(|V|+|E|))$. Defining the refined lower
bound $\ell_j^{(2)}=T_j$ for $j\in U_k^{(1)}$ and $\ell_j^{(2)}=M_j$ for
$j\in C\setminus U_k^{(1)}$, and letting $\ell_{(k)}^{(2)}$ denote its
$k$-th largest value over $C$, Stage~2 tightens the bracket to
$[\ell_j^{(2)},R_j]$ and forms
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_k^{(2)}&=\{a\in C : \ell_a^{(2)} > R_{(k+1)}\},\\
O_k^{(2)}&=\{b\in C : R_b < \ell_{(k)}^{(2)}\},\\
U_k^{(2)}&=C\setminus\bigl(I_k^{(2)}\cup O_k^{(2)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\eqtag[Stage 2 tree-refined certified screening partition]{3-14b}
\]
Monte Carlo estimation is invoked only when $U_k^{(2)}\ne\emptyset$, and
solely to resolve the remaining $k-|I_k^{(2)}|$ budget slots among residual
overlaps or ties within $U_k^{(2)}$.

\textit{Certification soundness.}
Under Assumptions~A1--A5, $M_j\le\ell_j^{(2)}\le\pi_j\le R_j$ holds for
every $j\in C$ (Equation~(3-13)). Write $\ell_j^{(1)}=M_j$. For either stage
$s\in\{1,2\}$, if $a\in I_k^{(s)}$, then $R_a\ge\ell_a^{(s)}>R_{(k+1)}$, so
the at least $|C|-k$ candidates $b\in C$ satisfying $R_b\le R_{(k+1)}$ are
all distinct from $a$ and obey
$\pi_b\le R_b\le R_{(k+1)}<\ell_a^{(s)}\le\pi_a$. At most $k-1$ candidates
can therefore match or exceed $\pi_a$, forcing $a\in\mathcal{T}_k^*$ for
every exact top-$k$ set regardless of tie-breaking. Dually, if
$b\in O_k^{(s)}$, then $\ell_b^{(s)}\le R_b<\ell_{(k)}^{(s)}$, so the at
least $k$ candidates $a\in C$ satisfying $\ell_a^{(s)}\ge\ell_{(k)}^{(s)}$
are all distinct from $b$ and obey
$\pi_a\ge\ell_a^{(s)}\ge\ell_{(k)}^{(s)}>R_b\ge\pi_b$, excluding $b$ from
every exact top-$k$ set $\mathcal{T}_k^*$. Because
$\ell_j^{(2)}\ge M_j$ holds pointwise, Stage~2 satisfies monotonic
refinement ($I_k^{(1)}\subseteq I_k^{(2)}$, $O_k^{(1)}\subseteq O_k^{(2)}$,
$U_k^{(2)}\subseteq U_k^{(1)}$) and $I_k^{(s)}\cap O_k^{(s)}=\emptyset$. To
prevent floating-point rounding from inducing spurious strict inequalities,
the software implementation enforces the declared tolerance
$\epsilon=10^{-12}$ from Section~\ref{reference-metrics}, requiring
$\ell_a^{(s)}>R_{(k+1)}+\epsilon$ for $I_k^{(s)}$ and
$R_b<\ell_{(k)}^{(s)}-\epsilon$ for $O_k^{(s)}$.

\textbf{Self-diagnosing bracket-width reliability.}
At zero additional asymptotic cost beyond Stage~1, the scalar pass produces
the nodewise bracket width
\[
W_j = R_j - M_j \qquad\text{for every }j\in V.
\eqtag[Zero-cost scalar bracket-width reliability indicator]{3-14c}
\]
Because $0\le R_j-\pi_j\le W_j$ holds unconditionally under
Assumptions~A1--A5 without computing $T_j$ or estimating $\pi_j$, $W_j$
operates as a self-diagnosing reliability indicator. Small $W_j$ certifies
tight pointwise agreement directly from the linear pass, whereas elevated
$W_j$ flags potential shared-ancestry amplification or ranking ambiguity
before committing compute to targeted tree bounds or Monte Carlo sampling.
Section~\ref{reference-metrics} pre-registers the empirical evaluation of
both Two-Stage Certified Top-$k$ Screening and $W_j$ as a predictor of
high-distortion and reversal-prone regimes.
