\section{Summary and Conclusion}\label{summary-and-conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the Study}

This study designed, formally specified, implemented, and evaluated the
\textbf{Graph-Based Bounded Risk Propagation Model (GBBRPM)}, a
modular adapter-to-core framework for static bounded receiving-state
prioritization on directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), together with its
\textbf{Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening} harness
and traceable evaluation engine. Rather than claiming a newly invented
algebraic operator, the study integrated the established
probabilistic-sum t-conorm \citep{klement2004} into a domain-decoupled,
fail-closed single-pass DAG architecture and tested it against matched
aggregation, restricted-tree, association-bound, and ancestry-aware
comparators under a frozen protocol
(\texttt{v2.0.0-protocol-freeze} $\to$ \texttt{v2.0.0-heldout-eval}).
Across $4,316$ primary benchmark scenarios ($88.86\text{ billion}$
edge-trials), $48$ independent $N=1{,}000{,}000$ precision audits,
$158$ computational scaling measurements, and $115$ real-domain
evaluations, the findings addressed all six research questions (RQ1--RQ6)
and Specific Objectives~1--6 as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Formal Architecture and Contract Specification (RQ1 /
Objective~1).} GBBRPM separated raw domain adapters from a shared
single-pass DAG core mapping bounded local disturbance $B_j \in [0,1]$,
relation-specific susceptibility $S_{ij} \in [0,1]$, and optional
transmission $\tau_{ij} \in [0,1]$ into a dimensionless receiving-state
index $R_j \in [0,1]$ via Equations~(3-1)--(3-5). Under independent
Bernoulli primitive assumptions (Assumptions~A1--A5), topological
induction and Harris--FKG positive association established the
conditional receiving-state bracket
$M_j \le T_j \le \pi_j \le R_j \le H_j$, which grounded both Two-Stage
Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening and the zero-cost scalar
reliability indicator $W_j = R_j - M_j$.
\item \textbf{Reproducible Engine and Traceable Prototype (RQ2 /
Objective~2).} The frozen evaluation engine and interactive research
prototype passed \texttt{88/88} core \texttt{pytest} checks,
\texttt{5/5} legacy analytical verification scripts, \texttt{76/76}
prototype functional checks, and \texttt{35/35} cryptographic SHA-256
protocol-freeze manifest verifications. Both layers enforced
fail-closed rejection of directed cycles, self-loops, non-finite inputs,
and ambiguous dual $S+L/C$ specifications while maintaining $0.0$
numerical discrepancy between the traceable DataFrame API and the CSR
execution kernel and exposing node-level, edge-contribution, and
bracket-width traces.
\item \textbf{Bracket Tightness, Comparator Ranking, and Certified
Screening (RQ3 / Objective~3).} Across $2,880$ held-out uniform-DAG
scenarios ($240$ independent graph replicates) with zero bracket
violations and zero false certifications across $8,640$ screening
evaluations, GBBRPM achieved mean upper slack $R_j - \pi_j = 0.0060$
(compared to tree lower slack $\pi_j - T_j = 0.0390$ and scalar lower
slack $\pi_j - M_j = 0.1065$). At the primary $f=0.20$ budget, GBBRPM
attained the lowest fixed-budget selection regret ($0.00742$), highest
captured activation mass ($50.59\%$), and highest rank correlation
($\rho = 0.9833$), outperforming $T_j$ ($0.1483$), clipped-sum $H_j$
($0.2889$, which saturated $\ge 0.95$ on $16.29\%$ of candidates vs.\
$1.49\%$ for $R_j$), the \citet{abbe2020} DAG lower bound ($0.2968$),
the ancestry diagnostic ($0.5590$), local-upstream ablation ($0.9185$),
and $M_j$ ($0.9332$) on $97.5\%$--$100.0\%$ of graph replicates.
Two-Stage Certified Screening increased deterministic top-$k$
resolution from $47.5\%$ (Stage~1) to $78.9\%$ (Stage~2) at $f=0.20$
($16.25\%$ full certification), delivering $2.00\times$ aggregate speedup
over full-graph $T_j$ and $18.6\times$ over $100\text{k}$ Monte Carlo.
Paired heterogeneous-weight experiments ($120$ evaluations) tightened
brackets further ($\Delta W = -0.00702$, $\Delta\mathrm{Regret} = -0.00112$,
$\Delta\mathrm{Res}_{S2} = +3.27\text{ pp}$), and $48$ independent
$1\text{M}$-trial audits verified $100.0\%$ agreement inside $100\text{k}$
Hoeffding intervals ($\max|\Delta\hat{\pi}| = 0.00475$, zero reversals).
\item \textbf{Shared-Ancestry Dependence, $W_j$ Self-Diagnosis, and
Ancestry Diagnostic Limits (RQ4 / Objective~4).} Across $560$ exact
matched branch controls, GBBRPM matched $\pi_j$ to machine precision
($1.11\times 10^{-16}$) on independent origins and collapsed exactly to
$R_j = \pi_j$ at deterministic shared origins ($b=1$), while exposing
one-sided upper slack up to $0.6499$ under uncertain shared origins
($0 < b < 1$). On held-out DAGs, shared-ancestry collapse generated
interval-resolved pairwise reversals in $696/2,880$ scenarios (pooled
rate $0.198\%$), concentrated almost entirely at $w \in \{0.50, 0.75\}$
and $B=0.50$. The scalar width $W_j = R_j - M_j$ proved effective as a
scenario-level reversal alarm (Spearman $\rho = 0.6769$, $100.0\%$
recall at $W_{\max} \ge 0.10$), but weak for within-graph nodewise
slack ordering ($\rho = 0.2286$) because $W_j$ combines small upper
slack with much larger legitimate independent multi-branch convergence.
The connected-component ancestry diagnostic worsened fixed-budget
ranking ($\mathrm{Regret}_{0.20}=0.5590$) by discarding genuine
cumulative multi-path contributions.
\item \textbf{Computational Scalability and Memory Tradeoffs (RQ5 /
Objective~5).} CSR GBBRPM propagation scaled near-linearly against
$|V|+|E|$ on both sparse DAGs ($|V|=20\dots 1000$: $0.058\to 3.27\text{ ms}$,
$b=1.045$, $R^2=0.9982$, peak heap $8.3\text{ KB}$) and dense DAGs
($|V|\le 250, |E|\le 7,781$: $b=1.022$, $R^2=0.9974$, $2.4\text{ KB}$),
whereas full-graph $T_j$ grew super-linearly ($b=1.853$,
$1,225.0\text{ ms}$ at $|V|=1000$). Against paired bit-packed
$100\text{k}$ Monte Carlo ($16.4$--$268.8\text{ ms}$,
$3.7$--$45.7\text{ MB}$), CSR GBBRPM achieved $879\times$--$1,103\times$
speedups and Two-Stage Certified Screening achieved
$17.1\times$--$28.0\times$ speedups.
\item \textbf{Real-Domain Instantiations and Eligibility Boundaries
(RQ6 / Objective~6).} All three domains passed Gate~A structural
readiness with zero invariant violations: the DPWH Cagayan de Oro
Bitan-ag--Kolambog drainage DAG ($11$ nodes, $11$ edges, $67$ Tier-2
runs) quantified real-topology reconvergence at \texttt{J\_AGORA\_CONF}
($\max R - \pi = 0.05108$, $98.8\%$ Stage-2 resolution);
\texttt{npm:express@4.18.2} ($71$ nodes, $128$ edges) verified root
risk $0.997528076171875$ at $\tau=1.00$ and isolated \texttt{qs}
($0.2500$) and \texttt{send} ($0.1875$) reconvergence while documenting
that all $7$ OSV advisory roots occupy the top-most dependent subgraph
($0/64$ non-advisory downstream nodes); and \texttt{Caltech SoCal
28-Bus} Component~2 ($12$-node arborescence, $43$ runs) confirmed exact
tree equivalence ($\max|R-\pi| = 2.22\times 10^{-16}$). None of the
three cases met Gate~B independent outcome validation (DPWH and Caltech
failed Gate~B; Express was Gate~B N/A).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusions}

Based on the confirmatory \texttt{v2.0.0-heldout-eval} evidence, the
study draws five conclusions with explicit scope boundaries:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{GBBRPM is a tight, saturation-resistant receiving-state
upper bound for sparse and moderately weighted DAGs.} Under the
declared independent-primitive reference, scalar complement-product
propagation achieves substantially lower fixed-budget regret and higher
rank fidelity than maximum, clipped-additive, local-only, local-upstream,
restricted-tree, \citet{abbe2020}, and naive ancestry-pruning
comparators while running in near-linear $O(|V|+|E|)$ time and
kilobyte-scale memory---two to three orders of magnitude faster than
vectorized $100\text{k}$ Monte Carlo.
\item \textbf{Shared-ancestry collapse is bounded, diagnosable, and
manageable without abandoning $O(|V|+|E|)$ screening.} Scalar local-state
updates cannot eliminate shared-origin overestimation on reconvergent
DAGs with uncertain sources and strong edge weights ($w \ge 0.50$),
where interval-resolved pairwise reversals occur at a small but nonzero
rate ($0.198\%$ pooled across held-out runs). However, pairing GBBRPM
with \textbf{Two-Stage Deterministic Certified Top-$k$ Screening}
certifies nearly four-fifths ($78.9\%$) of candidate top-$k$ decisions
with zero false certifications and zero Monte Carlo trials, while
$W_{\max}$ provides a zero-cost, $100\%$-recall scenario alarm for
identifying reversal-prone regimes.
\item \textbf{Heuristic ancestry pruning is not a drop-in improvement.}
Naive connected-component ancestry reduction discarded genuine
multi-branch accumulation and degraded selection regret by two orders
of magnitude relative to scalar $R_j$. Improving upon GBBRPM in
high-overlap regimes requires exact or tighter dependence-aware
bounding rather than ad-hoc path deletion.
\item \textbf{Coherent cross-domain instantiation does not equate to
operational validation.} The shared GBBRPM core cleanly accommodated
urban drainage, software dependency, and radial electrical topologies
under documented Gate~A mappings without pooling scores across domains.
Because DPWH drainage and Caltech electrical datasets lacked aligned
independent outcome records (Gate~B FAIL) and OSV advisories served as
software inputs rather than incident labels (Gate~B N/A), the present
study establishes \emph{methodological, algorithmic, and structural
mechanism validity}---not validated field-flooding prediction,
exploitation forecasting, or electrical fault prediction.
\item \textbf{Strict DAG acyclicity remains a hard applicability
boundary.} GBBRPM~v1 is unsuitable without modification for looped
stormwater grids, surcharged backwater hydraulics
(\hyperref[app:preliminary]{Appendix D}), meshed power grids, or cyclic
software dependency rings; enforcing fail-closed cycle rejection ensures
that these boundaries are surfaced transparently rather than hidden by
silent graph mutation.
\end{enumerate}
