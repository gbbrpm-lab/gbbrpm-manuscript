\section{Summary and Conclusion}\label{summary-and-conclusion}

\subsection{Overview}

This study designed and evaluated GBBRPM v1 as a bounded recursive model
for comparative node-risk prioritization in directed acyclic networks.
The evidence combines controlled architecture tests with three
domain-specific cases: drainage, software dependency, and electrical
networks. The cases intentionally support different claims and are not
presented as equivalent forms of outcome validation.

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

\subsubsection{Architecture Behavior}

Across the controlled N1--N5 experiments, GBBRPM preserved the declared
$[0,1]$ bounds and monotone response to increases in local disturbance
and susceptibility. Recursive current-state propagation, heterogeneous
edge conditions, disturbance location, and multi-source configuration
materially affected both risk magnitude and ranking. Randomized property
tests passed all 600 trials for boundedness, monotonicity, full local
disturbance, and zero-susceptibility gating.

Ranking robustness remained high under modest perturbations, although
agreement among the highest-priority nodes declined faster than the
overall Spearman ordering. The reconvergence diagnostic also showed that
shared ancestry can be counted through multiple paths, especially in N5.
This is a known boundary of the local multiplicative-complement
aggregator rather than evidence of probabilistic independence.

The sparse-DAG timing experiment was consistent with the expected
one-pass $O(|V|+|E|)$ implementation. Median execution time increased
from approximately 1.63 ms at 20 nodes to 16.65 ms at 1000 nodes under
the reported environment.

\subsubsection{Drainage Evidence and Hydraulic Boundary}

The drainage case supplied the most complete behavioral evaluation. The
controlled experiments demonstrated the effects of attenuation,
branching, convergence, reconvergence, local disturbance, susceptibility,
and multiple active sources. However, the 12-scenario SWMM comparison
produced negative mean rank association and low top-20\% overlap for
both maximum-depth and flooding outcomes. Hydraulic worsening also
occurred outside the model's strictly downstream region.

These findings do not support interpreting the GBBRPM index as a
hydraulic predictor. Instead, they identify a clear scope boundary:
GBBRPM represents lightweight downstream comparative propagation,
whereas SWMM represents hydraulic mechanisms such as backwater,
surcharge, ponding, and flow redistribution.

The mismatch does not identify the causal hydraulic mechanism in each
scenario or establish the usefulness of GBBRPM for drainage maintenance
prioritization. That intended use remains subject to separate validation
against relevant outcomes.

\subsubsection{Software-Dependency Evidence}

The Express 4.18.2 case instantiated the frozen core on a real topology
of 71 packages and 128 dependency edges, with seven evidence-backed
affected versions. Express root risk increased from 0.5000 to 0.9975
across the transmission sweep and reached rank one by $\tau=0.50$.
Ranking agreement with the $\tau=1$ reference remained high.

This result supports real-topology structural applicability and expected
sensitivity to transmission. It does not establish that the modeled
index is an exploit probability or a predictor of future incidents.

Reconverging dependencies also carry shared-origin influence into
multiple Express inputs. The local aggregator may therefore inflate the
root state through overlap; the software case does not quantify this
effect, and ranking stability does not establish unbiased magnitudes.

\subsubsection{Electrical-Network Evidence}

The full-network electrical stage verified import and baseline
preprocessing for 187 bus records, 177 active directed connections, and
269 channel files. The workflow handled 38 empty archive members without
terminating, reported mapping and missingness explicitly, identified 54
usable voltage channels, and measured 98.31\% overall timestamp coverage.

The Component 2 stage extended this evidence through a controlled
real-topology, measured-loading instantiation. Its observable model contained
12 nodes and 11 edges parameterized from 24 aligned phasor snapshots.
Across 43 deterministic scenarios, risks and edge contributions remained
bounded. Root severity, measured-load scaling, and uniform transmission
produced monotone increases in downstream risk. Equal disturbances at
different nodes produced different risk sums, with C2\_N02 highest at
1.417 because it bypassed the initial transformer attenuation while
retaining broad downstream reach. The all-internal-source case affected
10 of 12 nodes while remaining bounded at a maximum node risk of 0.599.

These findings support controlled electrical mechanism and sensitivity
claims, not event prediction. The measurements represent normal operation
and are not aligned with the requested switching-event outcomes;
electrical event-response validation therefore remains pending.

\subsubsection{Research Prototype Evidence}

The interactive prototype connected the frozen evaluator to validated
synthetic and imported data through a common schema. It exposed explicit
editing of $B$, $L$, $C$, and $\tau$, directed-network visualization,
risk coloring, edge-contribution tracing, and comparative rankings. The
prototype reproduced the frozen N5 outlet result, evaluated a controlled
imported graph correctly, rejected invalid endpoint and cyclic inputs,
and completed a production frontend build. Operational mode remained
inactive pending a provenance-complete agency dataset.

These findings support implementation feasibility and traceable model
interaction. They do not constitute usability evaluation, production
readiness, or operational decision validation.

\subsection{Answers to the Research Questions}

The combined evidence answers the six research questions within the
declared non-probabilistic and non-predictive scope:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
  \item Local disturbance, current upstream risk, relation-specific
  susceptibility, optional transmission, directed reachability, and
  multi-source input are integrated through a topologically ordered
  evaluator and the multiplicative-complement aggregation rule. The
  resulting state remains traceable to its local and incoming terms.
  \item The formulation preserved the declared $[0,1]$ bounds and
  monotone response in the controlled scenarios and all 600 randomized
  property trials. Comparative rankings also changed in the expected
  direction when source severity, edge susceptibility, and disturbance
  placement changed.
  \item The model responded materially to severity, susceptibility,
  topology, location, and multi-source configuration; retained high
  overall ranking agreement under modest perturbation; exposed reduced
  top-set stability at larger perturbations; and exhibited runtime growth
  consistent with one-pass $O(|V|+|E|)$ evaluation on the tested sparse
  DAGs.
  \item The frozen update rule was instantiated without redefinition in
  drainage, software-dependency, and electrical-network cases. The
  domain meanings and evidence sources changed, but the roles of $B$,
  $S$, $\tau$, $Q$, and $R$ remained fixed.
  \item The validated architecture was implemented as an interactive
  research prototype using a common input contract, graph validation,
  controlled parameter editing, the pinned model engine, and traceable
  network and ranking outputs. Functional checks reproduced preserved
  results and rejected invalid graphs, supporting implementation
  feasibility without implying production readiness.
  \item The domains and prototype provide unequal but complementary evidence: drainage
  supplies controlled behavior and a hydraulic-reference boundary;
  software supplies real-topology structural and transmission-sensitivity
  evidence; and electrical data supply importer verification plus
  controlled real-topology, measured-loading sensitivity evidence. None
  of these results establishes universal predictive validity, and the
  electrical case still lacks event-aligned independent outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusions}

Within the tested scope, the study concludes that:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
  \item GBBRPM v1 provides a deterministic, bounded, and interpretable
  recursive architecture for comparative risk prioritization on DAGs.
  \item Network structure and heterogeneous relation conditions can
  change prioritization beyond what local disturbance alone indicates.
  \item The model is broadly robust to modest input perturbations, but
  exact membership of the highest-priority set becomes less stable as
  uncertainty increases.
  \item The same frozen propagation rule can be instantiated coherently
  in the tested drainage, software, and electrical network structures.
  This supports cross-domain instantiability, not universal validity.
  \item The SWMM disagreement establishes that GBBRPM should not be used
  as a substitute for hydraulic simulation.
  \item The electrical evidence verifies topology and baseline
  preprocessing and demonstrates bounded, monotone controlled responses
  on Component 2 under measured loading. Event-centered validation cannot
  be claimed until temporally aligned inputs and independent outcomes are
  obtained.
  \item The prototype demonstrates that the frozen model can be exposed
  through a validated, interactive, and traceable workflow. It remains a
  research artifact rather than a production or autonomous decision
  system.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}

GBBRPM v1 is limited to directed acyclic graphs and one-pass propagation.
It does not model cycles, feedback, bidirectional influence, explicit
time evolution, or common-cause dependence correction. The bounded risk
state is a comparative index rather than a calibrated probability.
Domain-specific disturbance, susceptibility, and transmission mappings
remain dependent on the quality and defensibility of their input
evidence.

The drainage case uses controlled networks and simulated hydraulic
reference outcomes rather than a calibrated operational field study.
The software case demonstrates structural applicability and sensitivity
rather than observed incident prediction. The Component 2 electrical
suite injects controlled disturbances into a measured normal-operation
baseline; it does not contain observed event labels or outcomes aligned
with the requested windows. Component 2 was selected purposively using
observability, traceability, branching structure, and DAG compatibility,
not through random sampling; the 24-snapshot window supports descriptive
parameter instantiation rather than temporal or population-level
generalization. Its residual power is reported but not spatially
allocated, and one unobserved component edge is excluded. The three cases
therefore cannot be pooled as if they offered identical empirical
strength.

The prototype was verified for core functions and production compilation,
but not through formal user testing, accessibility evaluation, concurrent
multi-user operation, authentication, persistent storage, or deployment
hardening. Its operational-data mode remains inactive until a documented
adapter and provenance-complete dataset are available.
