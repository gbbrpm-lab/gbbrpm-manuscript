\section{Methodology}\label{methodology}

\subsection{Research Design and Study Phases}\label{research-design}

The study will follow a quantitative model-design and computational-evaluation
approach. It will formulate GBBRPM, implement the formulation in a deterministic
evaluation engine, prepare documented inputs, and assess the resulting behavior
and resource requirements. Its scientific emphasis will be the receiving-state
accuracy--cost tradeoff under a separately defined stochastic reference,
with controlled shared-origin dependence and task-matched comparisons.
Three separate domain cases will examine coherent instantiation of the same equations. An interactive prototype will expose the
engine's inputs, calculations, and comparative outputs. Figure~\ref{fig:unified_architecture}
in Chapter~1 summarizes the unified end-to-end architecture connecting these
domain adapters, fail-closed validation and topological DAG evaluation,
matched comparators, stochastic-reference and Two-Stage Deterministic Certified
Top-$k$ Screening harness, and traceable research artifacts.

The research will proceed through design, implementation, data preparation,
evaluation, and interpretation. Literature synthesis will inform the design
requirements rather than stand as a separate empirical-validation objective.
The model specification and evaluation protocol will be versioned before the
planned evaluation is executed. Implementation checks may reveal defects that
require correction; any resulting specification or protocol change will be
recorded and affected tests will be repeated.

\subsection{Design Requirements and Model Boundary}\label{design-requirements}

The formulation will address six requirements: a bounded non-probabilistic
node state; simultaneous representation of local and inherited disturbance;
direction-constrained influence; relation-specific modification; deterministic
DAG evaluation; and traceable inputs and outputs. Existing propagation
literature motivates separating the meaning of the propagated state from
the mechanisms used to update it
\citep{kempe2003,buldyrev2010,sheyner2002,munoz2017,petrone2018}.

The study will distinguish GBBRPM, the named mathematical model, from the
\textbf{GBBRPM evaluation engine}, its software implementation. The core
will specify how supplied bounded inputs are combined. The primary output
will rank accumulated receiving state. Inspection selection will be evaluated
only against a stated outcome, and intervention value will require a separate
before-and-after task. The stochastic reference will be an evaluation model
with its own primitive assumptions, not a replacement domain adapter. Domain adapters will
supply their meanings and calculations; an adapter will not be permitted
to silently replace the core update rule.

\paragraph{Justification for the DAG Scope.}
A domain case will be eligible only if it can provide defensible node and
edge meanings and a valid directed acyclic graph (DAG). Restricting GBBRPM v1
to finite DAGs rests on three structural and theoretical requirements:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \textit{Finite deterministic termination without cyclic double-counting:}
Acyclicity guarantees a single exact topological evaluation pass in
$O(|V|+|E|)$ time without arbitrary iteration limits, heuristic damping, or
self-reinforcing cyclic inflation.
\item \textit{Structural alignment with target domain networks:} Gravity-fed
branched urban drainage networks (including engineered diversions and
downstream confluences), acyclic software package dependency graphs, and
radial electrical distribution feeders naturally exhibit directional
upstream-to-downstream propagation.
\item \textit{Validity of topological induction for formal guarantees:} Both
global input monotonicity (Section~\ref{model-formulation}) and the
independent-primitive receiving-state bracket
$M_j\le T_j\le\pi_j\le R_j\le H_j$ via the Harris--FKG inequality
(Section~\ref{benchmark-bracket}) require inductively that no predecessor
state depends on its outgoing edge or downstream receiver.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Cycle-Handling and Non-Applicability Policy.}
A directed graph is not necessarily acyclic, and cyclic or bidirectional
physical systems will not be declared compatible merely because an importer
produces directed records. Crucially, \emph{converging flows are not cycles}:
a junction receiving multiple upstream branches (in-degree $\ge 2$, such as
the Bitan-ag/Agora confluence \texttt{J\_\allowbreak AGORA\_\allowbreak CONF},
PCSWMM node \texttt{J5}, in the DPWH urban drainage case) preserves strict
acyclicity and is handled natively by GBBRPM. By contrast, true directed
cycles---such as looped pipe rings, bidirectional pressure-driven hydraulic
backwater or surcharged flow reversals, and mutually recursive software
packages---violate Equation~(3-1) and trigger an explicit \emph{fail-closed
validation rejection}. The engine will never perform silent edge deletion,
heuristic cycle breaking, or unannounced graph condensation. Applying GBBRPM
v1 to a broader network that contains physical loops requires an external,
pre-documented acyclic sub-catchment or radial-feeder extraction in which
every excluded edge, consolidated bus, and resulting interpretation boundary
is explicitly logged (Section~\ref{domain-parameterization}). Native modeling
of cyclic feedback, hydraulic backwater, or strongly connected component
(SCC) condensation with redefined super-node semantics is explicitly out of
scope for GBBRPM v1 and is reserved for a separately specified future
extension.

\subsection{Detailed Formulation of GBBRPM}\label{model-formulation}

\paragraph{Formal Problem Representation and Complexity Summary.}
Given a validated finite DAG $G=(V,E)$, supplied bounded local disturbances
$B_i\in[0,1]$, relation-specific susceptibilities $S_{ij}\in[0,1]$ (direct or
adapter-derived), and transmission modifiers $\tau_{ij}\in[0,1]$ defining
effective edge weights $w_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}$, the deterministic core
problem is to compute the receiving-state vector
$\mathbf{R}=(R_j)_{j\in V}\in[0,1]^{|V|}$ in a single topological pass and
derive its descending comparative ranking. Within the separately defined
independent-primitive stochastic benchmark (Section~\ref{stochastic-reference}),
the evaluation problem additionally brackets each node's activation marginal
$\pi_j=\Pr(X_j=1)$ via $M_j\le T_j\le\pi_j\le R_j\le H_j$
(Section~\ref{benchmark-bracket}) and performs Two-Stage Deterministic
Certified Top-$k$ Screening over the zero-local-input candidate set
$C=\{j\in V: B_j=0\}$ (Section~\ref{certified-screening}).
Table~\ref{tab:complexity-summary} summarizes the worst-case time and space
complexities of GBBRPM, its comparators, screening stages, and reference
solvers.

\subsubsection{Graph, State, and Input Contract}\label{input-contract}

The proposed graph is
\[
G=(V,E),\qquad G\text{ is a directed acyclic graph}.
\eqtag{3-1}
\]
Nodes will represent domain entities, and an edge $(i,j)$ will represent
permitted influence from $i$ to $j$. The predecessor set is
$P(j)=\{i:(i,j)\in E\}$. Direction means modeled influence, which must be
justified for each instantiation; it need not be physical flow in every domain.

$R_i$ will be a continuous comparative index, not a calibrated probability.
Its interpretation will be conditional on the selected graph, scenario, and
normalization. Values from different domains will not be placed on a common
severity or likelihood scale merely because they lie in $[0,1]$.

$B_i$ will represent disturbance introduced directly at node $i$.
$S_{ij}$ will describe the response of a relation or receiving context.
$\tau_{ij}$ will describe transfer along that relation. The baseline will set
$\tau_{ij}=1$ where independently defensible heterogeneous values are unavailable.
A non-neutral uniform sweep will test attenuation, not empirical calibration.

\paragraph{Direct-Neighbor (Local-State) Assumption.}
By construction, the GBBRPM core update at any receiving node $j$ reads
\emph{only} its own local disturbance $B_j$, its incoming edge modifiers
$(S_{ij},\tau_{ij})$, and the scalar accumulated states $R_i$ of its immediate
predecessors $i\in P(j)$. The scalar engine maintains no path history or
upstream origin labels during propagation. This local-state design is what
enables single-pass $O(|V|+|E|)$ evaluation, while also establishing the exact
structural boundary where reconvergent paths sharing a common upstream ancestor
cannot be disentangled by the scalar update alone
(Section~\ref{dependence-boundary} and Section~\ref{ancestry-diagnostic}).

\subsubsection{Relation-Level Contribution and Identifiability}

For a directed edge, the model will use
\[
Q_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}R_i.
\eqtag{3-2}
\]
This specified rule will make the contribution zero when the upstream state
or either modifier is zero and will ensure that the contribution cannot exceed
the current upstream state. It is a bounded response construction, not
a physical law of transported load or energy. Branching will allow the same
upstream state to influence several receivers without interpreting it as a
conserved quantity that must be divided among outgoing edges.

\small
\textbf{Non-Conservative Transmission vs.\ Conserved Flow Apportionment.}
GBBRPM does \emph{not} split a fixed $100\%$ mass or flow budget across
outgoing edges ($\sum_{j:(i,j)\in E} f_{ij}=1$). Instead, each outgoing edge
$(i,j)$ applies \emph{bounded influence scaling} independently via
$Q_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}R_i\in[0,1]$. For example, if $R_i=0.60$ feeds two
branches with effective weights $w_{ij_1}=0.50$ and $w_{ij_2}=0.40$, the
resulting contributions are $Q_{ij_1}=0.30$ and $Q_{ij_2}=0.24$ without
forcing outgoing fractions to sum to $100\%$. Conversely, when multiple
upstream contributions enter a single receiver $j$, they are never summed
linearly; they are combined with local disturbance $B_j$ through the bounded
complement-product rule $R_j=1-(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij})\in[0,1]$.
\end{minipage}}\par}
\medskip

The effective edge weight is $w_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}$. For fixed topology
and local disturbances, any admissible factor pairs with the same product
give the same node outputs. Separate interpretation of susceptibility and
transmission will therefore require independent measurements or justified
constructions; risk outputs alone do not identify both factors.

\subsubsection{Aggregation Design and Its Rationale}

The complement of the receiving-node state is specified as
\[
c_j=(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij}),\qquad R_j=1-c_j.
\eqtag{3-3}
\]
Thus the proposed update is
\[
R_j=1-(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij}).
\eqtag{3-4}
\]
The local complement and incoming complements jointly determine the
remaining modeled margin. Equation~(3-4) is exactly the associative
extension of the established probabilistic-sum t-conorm to the local
input and all incoming contributions \citep{klement2004}. The name
GBBRPM will identify the specified graph-index architecture rather than
a new aggregation operator. The chosen non-probabilistic interpretation
does not confer probabilistic independence or event-union semantics,
and does not itself establish a new mathematical mechanism.

The study will explain this choice relative to alternative bounded
aggregators. Sum with clipping admits cumulative contributions but reaches
its cap directly; a maximum retains only the strongest input; and an
arithmetic mean can fall when a weak input is added. The selected product
complement permits several nonzero contributions to increase the state
without exceeding one. This rationale concerns required mathematical
behavior; it does not establish superior physical realism. The planned
matched-aggregation comparison in Section~\ref{aggregation-comparison}
will assess that selection explicitly. RIPA's current-state modifiers
and leaky noisy-OR's background-plus-parent form will be compared as
close prior art \citep{faraji2026,fallet2012}; their domain-specific
probability or risk meanings will not be silently replaced to claim
a matched performance benchmark.

\subsubsection{Initialization, Recursion, and Output}

For a node with no predecessors, the empty product is one, giving
\[
P(j)=\emptyset\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_j=B_j.
\eqtag{3-5}
\]
A source node may therefore contain local disturbance. Its risk is not
automatically one. An isolated node follows the same rule.

Nodes will be evaluated in a topological ordering. All predecessor states
will be available before a receiver is computed. Each newly computed
state will immediately become the upstream state for later relations;
the engine will not substitute the original $B_i$ for an accumulated $R_i$.
Disconnected components will be evaluated independently.

\subsubsection{Analytical Properties and Dependence Boundary}\label{dependence-boundary}

The operator's basic boundedness and monotonicity are established
properties \citep{klement2004}. The following arguments will confirm
their consequences for this specified recursion, not claim a new theorem
about t-conorms. Boundedness follows because every term in Equation~(3-4) belongs to
$[0,1]$. For an incoming contribution $x_k=Q_{kj}$,
\[
\frac{\partial R_j}{\partial x_k}
=(1-B_j)\prod_{i\ne k}(1-x_i)\ge0,\qquad
\frac{\partial R_j}{\partial B_j}=\prod_i(1-x_i)\ge0.
\eqtag{3-6}
\]
These expressions establish local monotonicity. On a fixed DAG, global
monotonicity with respect to independently increased $B$, $S$, or $\tau$
follows by induction over topological order. This argument assumes the
other primitive inputs and parameter-construction rules are held fixed;
it does not guarantee unchanged rankings.

If $B_j=1$ or any incoming $Q_{ij}=1$, the receiving state is one.
A zero modifier gates its edge. By contrast, $S_{ij}=1$ alone leaves
$Q_{ij}=\tau_{ij}R_i$ and does not guarantee $R_j=1$.
The clipped $L/C$ adapter can saturate an edge's susceptibility before
the network's states saturate.

Under the Direct-Neighbor (Local-State) Assumption stated in
Section~\ref{input-contract}, the update at receiver $j$ reads only the
scalar states $R_i$ of immediate predecessors $i\in P(j)$ without retaining
path history or ancestor identities. Consequently, when one upstream
disturbance splits along multiple branches and reconverges---as occurs at the
Agora diversion confluence \texttt{J\_\allowbreak AGORA\_\allowbreak CONF}
in the DPWH drainage network (Section~\ref{domain-parameterization}) and along
shared \texttt{qs}/\texttt{send} paths in Express~4.18.2---incoming
contributions share ancestry, yet Equation~(3-4) combines their collapsed
scalar values as if they were separate inputs. Boundedness therefore does not
establish dependence correction. This structural boundary is investigated
explicitly through the matched shared-versus-independent branch controls
(Section~\ref{dependence-controls}) and the prospective ancestry-aware
diagnostic (Section~\ref{ancestry-diagnostic}), rather than hidden behind a
stable ranking or a high receiving-node state.

\subsection{Evaluation Engine and Prototype Design}\label{engine-design}

\subsubsection{Per-Scenario Engine Algorithm}

The engine will use node records and directed adjacency lists. For each
scenario it will perform the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Validate unique node identifiers, edge endpoints, parameter types,
finite values, admissible bounds, and the declared input representation.
\item Reject self-loops, duplicate directed edges, and cyclic graphs.
Generate and retain a topological order for valid input.
\item Obtain $S_{ij}$ directly or derive it through the declared adapter.
Set neutral $\tau_{ij}=1$ only where the contract permits an omitted value.
\item For each node in topological order, compute all incoming $Q_{ij}$,
apply Equation~(3-4), and retain the node and contribution trace.
\item Rank the resulting node states with the declared tie policy and
export outputs together with scenario and engine-version metadata.
\end{enumerate}

The interface will not infer missing disturbances from graph position.
Node $B$ and edge $S$ or its complete primitive pair will be required.
An explicit zero disturbance will be distinguished from an unavailable
observation. If both direct $S$ and an $L/C$ pair are provided for an edge,
the final contract will reject the ambiguous representation. Capacity
must be positive, load nonnegative, and units compatible.

Input violations will produce a reasoned validation failure instead of
silently clipping malformed inputs. Clipping will apply only to the
declared susceptibility mapping. Floating-point behavior near zero and
saturation will be checked against high-precision worked cases. The
engine will report the numerical tolerance used by its tests and ranking
policy; any change to the arithmetic implementation will require
regression comparison with the specified equations.

\subsubsection{Drainage Instantiation}

Nodes and directed relations will represent engineered urban drainage
junctions or outfalls and permitted gravity-fed downstream reaches. A
load-to-capacity adapter will derive relation susceptibility via
\[
u_{ij}=\frac{L_{ij}}{C_{ij}},\qquad S_{ij}=\min(1,u_{ij}),
\quad L_{ij}\ge0,\ C_{ij}>0.
\eqtag{3-7}
\]
To supply verified engineering inputs rather than synthetic placeholders, the
study acquired official drainage records from the Department of Public Works
and Highways Regional Office~X under Freedom of Information release
\texttt{FOI-REQ-2026-7383} (October 22, 2026) \citep{dpwh2026foi}, anchored
by the UP PLANADES \emph{Master Plan and Feasibility Study of Urban Drainage
Improvement and Related Works in Cagayan de Oro City} (Contract ID
\texttt{15K00003}, August 20, 2016) \citep{upplanades2016}.

\paragraph{Verified DPWH Provenance and Gate A / Gate B Triage.}
From the UP PLANADES hydraulic tables and PCSWMM network profiles, the study
constructed the reproducible case
\texttt{drainage\_\allowbreak dpwh\_\allowbreak cdo\_\allowbreak bitanag\_\allowbreak kolambog\_v1},
which passes \textbf{Gate~A (Real-Data DAG Instantiation and Mechanism Study)}.
The extracted network contains 11 nodes (three headwater sources, six
intermediate/diversion/confluence junctions, and two coastal outfalls) and 11
directed gravity-flow reaches forming a validated single weakly connected DAG
with zero self-loops and zero duplicate edges. Documented $Q_{25}$ and PCSWMM
discharges $L_{ij}$ and conduit capacities $C_{ij}$ ($\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm{s}$),
independently cross-checked against Manning's conveyance equation within

susceptibilities $S_{ij}\in[0.4639,0.8471]$ (mean $0.6417$, zero saturated
edges at $1.0\times$ load), with no dual $S$ column stored. Crucially, the
topology captures an authentic real-world \emph{shared-ancestry reconvergence
diamond}: flow splits at the Agora Street diversion tap
(\texttt{J\_\allowbreak AGORA\_\allowbreak SPLIT}, PCSWMM \texttt{J16}) into
the main trunk (\texttt{BCI-1B\_\allowbreak POST} $\to$
\texttt{BCI-1A\_\allowbreak UP}) and the twin $3.0\,\mathrm{m}\times 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$

\[

\sqrt{P_{ij,t}^{\,2}+Q_{ij,t}^{\,2}}.
\eqtag{3-8}
\]
The magnitude of mean $P$ and $Q$ will not be substituted for this mean

$3V_{\mathrm{phase}}I_{\mathrm{rated}}/1000$

supply electrical susceptibility.

as the selected input.

Independent switching-event analysis will be conditional on event-aligned
measurements and outcomes. Existing normal-operation coverage does not
provide those outcomes. Restricted raw measurements and credentials will
not be included in the public manuscript package.

\subsubsection{Matched Bounded-Aggregation Comparison}
\label{aggregation-comparison}

The study will separately compare the maximum, probabilistic-sum, and
bounded-sum t-conorms \citep{klement2004}. For a bounded input vector
$\mathbf{x}$, their definitions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}_{M}(\mathbf{x})&=\max_k x_k,\qquad
\mathcal{A}_{P}(\mathbf{x})=1-\prod_k(1-x_k),\\
\mathcal{A}_{L}(\mathbf{x})&=\min\left(1,\sum_k x_k\right).
\end{aligned}
\eqtag{3-9}
\]
The input vector at each receiver will contain its local $B_j$ and
every incoming contribution. Graph, $B$, $S$, $\tau$, and topological
order will be held fixed across conditions. Two complementary analyses
will be performed: an operator-only comparison using identical supplied
contribution vectors, and an end-to-end comparison in which each variant
recomputes all upstream states recursively with its own aggregator.
This prevents a comparison variant from incorrectly inheriting states
computed by the GBBRPM baseline.

For illustration, inputs $(0,0.5,0.5)$ give $0.5$, $0.75$, and $1.0$
under maximum, probabilistic sum, and bounded sum respectively. These
are algebraic examples, not new experimental findings. A chain with
one source disturbance and no intermediate local disturbance will
provide an agreement control because each receiver has only one
nonzero input. Independent convergence, multiple local-plus-incoming
terms, and reconvergence will expose differing accumulation behavior.

The comparison will use the documented N1--N5 configurations and their
severity and source-condition sweeps. Per-node paired differences,
terminal states, saturation fraction, Spearman agreement, and
tie-aware top-$k$ overlap will be reported. Saturation fraction will
count nodes with $R\ge1-10^{-12}$ and will be labeled as a numerical
reporting criterion. Constant-rank cases will follow the undefined
correlation policy in Section~\ref{analysis-reproducibility}.
Source count and reconvergence will be reported separately.

All three operators are bounded and monotone; those properties alone
will not identify a preferred choice. The analysis will characterize
accumulation, saturation, and ranking tradeoffs under the declared
index semantics. Improved physical or operational prioritization will
require an independent reference. Diagnostic variants will be separately
versioned in the experiment runner and counted outside the historical
136-run suite; they will not overwrite the frozen GBBRPM baseline.

\subsection{Properties, Sensitivity, Robustness, and Dependence}
\label{behavioral-evaluation}

Analytical arguments will be complemented by implementation tests for
zero disturbance, isolated nodes, chains, independent convergence,
reconvergence, zero edge modifiers, and saturated local disturbance.
Randomized admissible DAG/input cases will test the implementation against
the declared properties. Passing finitely many tests will not replace
the analytical argument.

Sensitivity tests will vary severity, source location, susceptibility,
uniform transmission, intermediate disturbance, and source combinations,
holding unrelated inputs fixed. Outputs will include node and terminal
states, maximum and mean risk, risk sum, and node counts above an
explicit reporting cutoff. A cutoff will be an analytical choice rather
than a physical failure threshold.

The planned robustness protocol will use 600 trials at each relative
perturbation magnitude $\delta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ with seed 41.
For this protocol, each edge susceptibility will be independently perturbed
as $S'_{ij}=\min(1,\max(0,S_{ij}(1+\epsilon_{ij})))$,
where $\epsilon_{ij}$ is uniform on $[-\delta,\delta]$.
Topology, disturbances, and transmission will remain fixed.
This stated protocol will be versioned; earlier outputs will not be
assumed identical if their generation conventions differ.

\subsubsection{Specified Ancestry-Aware Diagnostic and Its Evaluation}
\label{ancestry-diagnostic}

The prospective diagnostic will assign each node a set of local-origin labels.
A positive local term will introduce that node's own label; a positive incoming
contribution will carry the union of its predecessor's labels. At each receiver,
positive incoming contributions will be vertices in an overlap graph, joined
when their label sets intersect. Connected components will define groups,
including transitive overlap; within each group only the largest contribution
will be retained. The receiver's local term and these group maxima will be
combined by complement product. The variant will compute its own upstream
states and carry union label sets in topological order. This definition will
be versioned prospectively; it will not retroactively authenticate historical
outputs whose exact grouping convention differs.

The grouping may merge partially overlapping lineages that also contain
independent origins, and may discard independent edge opportunities from a
certain common source. The matched controls in Section~\ref{dependence-controls}
will test these failure cases. Reference error and fixed-budget selection will
be reported alongside ordinary GBBRPM and the restricted-tree comparator,
not merely the difference between
variants. Runtime, peak memory, lineage-set sizes, and pairwise overlap checks
will also be recorded. With $K$ origin labels and in-degree $d_j$
at receiver $j$, a straightforward set implementation can require
$O(K\sum_j d_j^2)$ overlap work in addition to propagation and label storage;
it will not inherit the scalar core's linear-time claim.

The protocol will not promise a successful correction. The diagnostic may
improve some regimes and worsen others. Any newly designed extension will
require a revised specification, development evaluation, and unused evaluation
cases; it will not be tuned on the held-out reference outcomes and then
reported as independently validated. Findings about this diagnostic will not
be generalized to all dependence-aware methods.

\subsection{Computational and Input-Cost Assessment}
\label{computational-scalability}

The lightweight claim will be assessed as a resource and representation
tradeoff. With adjacency-list storage and constant-cost edge operations,
DAG checking, topological ordering, and propagation require
\[
O(|V|+|E|),
\quad\text{whereas complete comparison sorting adds }O(|V|\log|V|).
\eqtag{3-18}
\]
Graph and stored contribution-trace memory will be $O(|V|+|E|)$.
The claim applies to one scenario with prepared inputs. Domain adapters,
data retrieval, many repeated scenarios, and interface rendering have
additional costs.

Sparse DAG sizes will initially include
$|V|\in\{20,50,100,250,500,1000\}$ with recorded edge counts, followed
by a denser-series stress test at 20, 50, 100, and 250 nodes with
$|E|=\lfloor0.25|V|(|V|-1)/2\rfloor$. A seeded random topological
ordering will restrict all edges to forward pairs, and unique pairs
will be selected up to the declared edge count. Sparse cases will target
three edges per node. Graphs and bounded inputs will be fixed by seed
41. Five warm-up runs and 30 measured repetitions per
configuration will provide median, interquartile range, and raw timings.
Graph validation/order, propagation, ranking, and total engine evaluation
will be timed separately, alongside peak memory under a stated measurement
boundary. Hardware, operating system, language, library versions, and
timer/memory instrumentation will be recorded.

The same prepared inputs will be used for relevant local, uniform, structural,
aggregation, restricted-tree, and recursion-ablation implementations. The ancestry-aware variant
will be timed separately with label preparation included. Restricted-tree
construction and per-target calculation will also be timed, including all
requested targets; it will not be compared as if its preprocessing were free. Simpler baselines may
remain cheaper. Reference comparisons will state trial count and sampling
precision. They will time one full $N=100{,}000$-trial solve per general-DAG
scenario and repeat the solve five times on one preselected graph per size
(forward-edge family, seed 1001, $w=0.5$, initial $B=0.5$). Deterministic methods will retain the
30-repetition protocol on those same graphs. Reference preparation, simulation,
and ranking will be separated, and batching/vectorization choices will be
reported. A one-pass index and repeated probabilistic estimation will not be
claimed to provide identical fidelity simply because they share inputs. A required-input inventory will separately list observed and
derived quantities, adapter effort, and missingness requirements.
A smaller core will not imply easy data acquisition.

Higher-fidelity comparison will document the modeled quantities, omitted
mechanisms, required fields, and workflow costs. A quantitative SWMM speed
claim will be made only if a separately documented paired benchmark is
performed with declared scenarios, simulation duration, solver settings,
hardware, and timing boundaries. A hydraulic simulation and a static
comparative-index evaluation remain different tasks.

Cross-case synthesis will examine defensible mappings, DAG eligibility,
core-rule consistency, computational requirements, and evidence limits.
Domain scores, correlations, and physical outcomes will not be pooled
as interchangeable performance measures. A common equation does not
establish common calibration.

\subsection{Prototype Functional Verification}\label{prototype-verification}

Functional verification will compare the recorded prototype revision with
the versioned engine on the N1--N5 fixtures, valid imports, and malformed
inputs. Checks will cover reference states within declared tolerance, trace coverage
and deterministic values, receiving-state reconstruction, and controlled
rejection of invalid graphs or non-finite numeric inputs.

Replay checks will assess predecessor-settlement order, concurrent
branches, input non-mutation, agreement of settled readouts with returned
quantities, and invariance of numerical results across display modes.
Automated and manual browser checks will inspect the supported controls and
graph layout. The detailed checklist and evidence-recording requirements
appear in Appendix
\hyperref[app:prototype-contract]{E}. These
checks will establish functional consistency in the tested configuration; they
will not establish usability, operational utility, or deployment readiness.

\subsection{Analysis, Evidence Synthesis, and Reproducibility}
\label{analysis-reproducibility}

\subsubsection{Metrics and Numerical Reporting}

Paired scenario differences will be reported at node and network level.
Spearman correlation will use average ranks for tied standing; a constant
rank vector will be reported as undefined rather than assigned a favorable
correlation. Numerical tie grouping will use a declared absolute tolerance
of $10^{-12}$, with each descending group's highest score as its anchor.

Top-$k$ sets will use the nominal cutoff
$k=\min(3,|V|)$ or $k=\max(1,\lceil0.20|V|\rceil)$, depending on the
declared metric. All scores within the numerical tolerance of the cutoff
score will be included. The resulting set can exceed nominal $k$.
Jaccard similarity will be
$J(A,B)=|A\cap B|/|A\cup B|$.
Top-three and top-20\% measures will be labeled separately.
These descriptive overlap conventions will be applied consistently to
planned runs and will not be retroactively attributed to every historical
table. Fixed-budget reference evaluation will instead select exactly $k$
nodes using the shared, outcome-independent tie permutations in
Section~\ref{reference-metrics}. Expanded cutoff sets will not be used to
claim an advantage at a fixed inspection budget.

Boundary and analytical tests will assess formal correctness. Comparator
differences without a reference will assess added information dependence;
reference-based errors and selection metrics will assess accuracy under the
specified stochastic process. Resource measures will assess cost and domain
analyses will support only their declared case-specific claims. Output disagreement alone will not establish
better prioritization.

\subsubsection{Provenance and Acquisition Contingencies}

The record will include input hashes, source versions, engine revision,
scenario settings, random seeds, trial counts, environment, and output
files. Reconstructed N1--N5 fixtures will be identified separately from
the earlier fixture inputs and will be versioned for reproducible reruns.
The original Express node and edge CSVs are available and match the
SHA-256 hashes in the historical output manifest. The software topology
and electrical measured loading will remain real-data inputs despite
the preliminary status of their experiments; input verification does
not establish independent operational validation.

The generated-DAG benchmark will use seeds 41--45 for development and
1001--1020 for evaluation after the simulator, diagnostic, metrics, exclusions,
and reporting protocol are frozen. The precision-audit subset will also be fixed.
Development and evaluation summaries will
be reported separately. The analytically specified branch examples will be
mechanism controls, not unseen confirmatory discoveries. Any amendment after
evaluation outcomes are inspected will be recorded and will require new
unused cases for a further confirmatory claim.

Previously examined scenarios will remain exploratory. Any new
confirmatory evaluation will specify its criteria and independent data
before inspecting outcomes. If suitable agency records or event-aligned
electrical evidence are unavailable at the acquisition checkpoint, the
study will report that limitation and review the permitted scope with the
adviser. Missing field records will not be replaced with invented
operational observations.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and One-Command Replication Contract.}
Before any held-out evaluation scenario (seeds 1001--1020) is executed, the
frozen specification, core engine, benchmark harness, and scenario/seed
configuration will be locked in version control under Git release tag
\texttt{v2.0.0-protocol-freeze} together with a pinned Python dependency
lockfile and an immutable seed manifest (covering development seeds 41--45,
held-out seeds 1001--1020, robustness seed 41, and tie-permutation seed 140).
A single deterministic command
(\texttt{scripts/generate\_\allowbreak paper\_\allowbreak outputs.py}) will
rebuild all thesis and publication tables and figures directly from the
frozen result directory and emit a cryptographic
\texttt{paper\_\allowbreak outputs\_\allowbreak manifest.json} containing
SHA-256 digests for every input snapshot, script revision, and generated
output artifact (recording external source-archive hashes for protected DPWH
and Caltech payloads without redistributing restricted raw files). To verify
end-to-end reproducibility prior to manuscript finalization, an independent
team verifier (\texttt{DJA}) will execute the frozen one-command pipeline
inside a clean virtual environment and confirm bitwise SHA-256 parity against
\texttt{paper\_\allowbreak outputs\_\allowbreak manifest.json}; any hash
discrepancy will trigger a logged defect audit before results are reported.

\subsection{Timeline of the Study}\label{study-timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} gives a provisional calendar for completing the
revised proposal and its planned evaluation, complementing the
module-by-module software implementation and ownership plan in
Table~\ref{tab:implementation-plan}. The model formulation,
prototype, and exploratory experiments already exist; the opening activity
identifies work completed before W1 without assigning its original duration.
Their record will be consolidated during proposal revision.
Literature review, proposal revision, and data follow-up will continue while
the team prepares for the proposal presentation and addresses adviser comments.

The subsequent phases will finalize the specification and engine, validate
the exact and Monte Carlo references, implement comparators, and freeze the
protocol before held-out evaluation. The W8 data checkpoint will review
available domain evidence, mapping completeness, and eligibility of the
verified Express snapshot for the planned benchmark. A
computational pilot during reference development will assess whether the
prospective scenario inventory is feasible. Any reduction will be documented
and frozen before evaluation outcomes, as specified in
Section~\ref{dependence-controls}. Mechanism comparisons will be followed by
cost and separate domain analyses; prototype checks and writing will overlap
these phases. Adviser review and revision buffers will precede and follow the
final defense. New evaluation-stage protocol changes will require unused
cases under Section~\ref{analysis-reproducibility}.

The 24-week duration remains a planning placeholder. If W1 begins on
October 12, 2026, W24 ends on March 28, 2027, and the W8 checkpoint falls
at the start of the week of November 30, 2026. Defense, submission, and event
dates will be confirmed separately. Conditional dissemination may extend
beyond the core period; its dashed tail is not an additional committed
research phase. Data unavailability will narrow the supported domain claims
rather than block the independently specified controlled benchmark.
