\section{Methodology}\label{methodology}

\subsection{Research Design and Study Phases}\label{research-design}

The study will follow a quantitative model-design and computational-evaluation
approach. It will formulate GBBRPM, implement the formulation in a deterministic
evaluation engine, prepare documented inputs, and assess the resulting behavior
and resource requirements. Three separate domain cases will examine coherent
instantiation of the same equations. An interactive prototype will expose the
engine's inputs, calculations, and comparative outputs.

The research will proceed through design, implementation, data preparation,
evaluation, and interpretation. Literature synthesis will inform the design
requirements rather than stand as a separate empirical-validation objective.
The model specification and evaluation protocol will be versioned before the
planned evaluation is executed. Implementation checks may reveal defects that
require correction; any resulting specification or protocol change will be
recorded and affected tests will be repeated.

\subsection{Design Requirements and Model Boundary}\label{design-requirements}

The formulation will address six requirements: a bounded non-probabilistic
node state; simultaneous representation of local and inherited disturbance;
direction-constrained influence; relation-specific modification; deterministic
DAG evaluation; and traceable inputs and outputs. Existing propagation
literature motivates separating the meaning of the propagated state from
the mechanisms used to update it
\citep{kempe2003,buldyrev2010,sheyner2002,munoz2017,petrone2018}.

The study will distinguish GBBRPM, the named mathematical model, from the
\textbf{GBBRPM evaluation engine}, its software implementation. The core
will specify how supplied bounded inputs are combined. Domain adapters will
supply their meanings and calculations; an adapter will not be permitted
to silently replace the core update rule.

A domain case will be eligible only if it can provide defensible node and
edge meanings and a valid DAG. A directed graph is not necessarily acyclic.
Cyclic or bidirectional physical systems will not be declared compatible
merely because an importer produces directed records. Any extracted
subgraph, consolidation, or excluded edge will be documented together
with its effect on the interpretation boundary. Modeling feedback would
require a separately specified extension.

\subsection{Detailed Formulation of GBBRPM}\label{model-formulation}

\subsubsection{Graph, State, and Input Contract}

The proposed graph is
\[
G=(V,E),\qquad G\text{ is a directed acyclic graph}.
\eqtag{3-1}
\]
Nodes will represent domain entities, and an edge $(i,j)$ will represent
permitted influence from $i$ to $j$. The predecessor set is
$P(j)=\{i:(i,j)\in E\}$. Direction means modeled influence, which must be
justified for each instantiation; it need not be physical flow in every domain.

$R_i$ will be a continuous comparative index, not a calibrated probability.
Its interpretation will be conditional on the selected graph, scenario, and
normalization. Values from different domains will not be placed on a common
severity or likelihood scale merely because they lie in $[0,1]$.

$B_i$ will represent disturbance introduced directly at node $i$.
$S_{ij}$ will describe the response of a relation or receiving context.
$\tau_{ij}$ will describe transfer along that relation. The baseline will set
$\tau_{ij}=1$ where independently defensible heterogeneous values are unavailable.
A non-neutral uniform sweep will test attenuation, not empirical calibration.

\subsubsection{Relation-Level Contribution and Identifiability}

For a directed edge, the model will use
\[
Q_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}R_i.
\eqtag{3-2}
\]
This specified rule makes the contribution zero when the upstream state
or either modifier is zero and ensures that the contribution cannot exceed
the current upstream state. It is a bounded response construction, not
a physical law of transported load or energy. Branching will allow the same
upstream state to influence several receivers without interpreting it as a
conserved quantity that must be divided among outgoing edges.

The effective edge weight is $w_{ij}=S_{ij}\tau_{ij}$. For fixed topology
and local disturbances, any admissible factor pairs with the same product
give the same node outputs. Separate interpretation of susceptibility and
transmission will therefore require independent measurements or justified
constructions; risk outputs alone do not identify both factors.

\subsubsection{Aggregation Design and Its Rationale}

The complement of the receiving-node state is specified as
\[
c_j=(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij}),\qquad R_j=1-c_j.
\eqtag{3-3}
\]
Thus the proposed update is
\[
R_j=1-(1-B_j)\prod_{i\in P(j)}(1-Q_{ij}).
\eqtag{3-4}
\]
The local complement and incoming complements jointly determine the
remaining modeled margin. This construction is an algebraic choice for a
bounded saturating index. Its resemblance to noisy-OR does not confer
probabilistic independence or event-union semantics.

The study will explain this choice relative to alternative bounded
aggregators. Sum with clipping admits cumulative contributions but reaches
its cap directly; a maximum retains only the strongest input; and an
arithmetic mean can fall when a weak input is added. The selected product
complement permits several nonzero contributions to increase the state
without exceeding one. This rationale concerns required mathematical
behavior; it does not establish superior physical realism.

\subsubsection{Initialization, Recursion, and Output}

For a node with no predecessors, the empty product is one, giving
\[
P(j)=\emptyset\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_j=B_j.
\eqtag{3-5}
\]
A source node may therefore contain local disturbance. Its risk is not
automatically one. An isolated node follows the same rule.

Nodes will be evaluated in a topological ordering. All predecessor states
will be available before a receiver is computed. Each newly computed
state will immediately become the upstream state for later relations;
the engine will not substitute the original $B_i$ for an accumulated $R_i$.
Disconnected components will be evaluated independently.

Figure~\ref{fig:generic-node-example} illustrates these mechanisms.
Appendix~\ref{app:worked-example} provides a complete controlled calculation.
The schematic and calculation explain the model; they are not independent
domain validation.

\input{figures/fig_generic_node_example}

Final $R_i$ values will be sorted in descending order. Ties will represent
equal modeled standing under the declared numerical policy rather than
receive an unsupported decision priority. Ranking is a derived output,
not an additional propagation mechanism.

\subsubsection{Analytical Properties and Dependence Boundary}

Boundedness follows because every term in Equation~(3-4) belongs to
$[0,1]$. For an incoming contribution $x_k=Q_{kj}$,
\[
\frac{\partial R_j}{\partial x_k}
=(1-B_j)\prod_{i\ne k}(1-x_i)\ge0,\qquad
\frac{\partial R_j}{\partial B_j}=\prod_i(1-x_i)\ge0.
\eqtag{3-6}
\]
These expressions establish local monotonicity. On a fixed DAG, global
monotonicity with respect to independently increased $B$, $S$, or $\tau$
follows by induction over topological order. This argument assumes the
other primitive inputs and parameter-construction rules are held fixed;
it does not guarantee unchanged rankings.

If $B_j=1$ or any incoming $Q_{ij}=1$, the receiving state is one.
A zero modifier gates its edge. By contrast, $S_{ij}=1$ alone leaves
$Q_{ij}=\tau_{ij}R_i$ and does not guarantee $R_j=1$.
The clipped $L/C$ adapter can saturate an edge's susceptibility before
the network's states saturate.

When one disturbance splits and reconverges, incoming contributions can
share ancestry. Equation~(3-4) will still combine them separately.
Boundedness therefore does not establish dependence correction. This
limitation will be evaluated explicitly rather than hidden by a stable
ranking or a high receiving-node state.

\subsection{Evaluation Engine and Prototype Design}\label{engine-design}

\subsubsection{Per-Scenario Engine Algorithm}

The engine will use node records and directed adjacency lists. For each
scenario it will perform the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Validate unique node identifiers, edge endpoints, parameter types,
finite values, admissible bounds, and the declared input representation.
\item Reject self-loops, duplicate directed edges, and cyclic graphs.
Generate and retain a topological order for valid input.
\item Obtain $S_{ij}$ directly or derive it through the declared adapter.
Set neutral $\tau_{ij}=1$ only where the contract permits an omitted value.
\item For each node in topological order, compute all incoming $Q_{ij}$,
apply Equation~(3-4), and retain the node and contribution trace.
\item Rank the resulting node states with the declared tie policy and
export outputs together with scenario and engine-version metadata.
\end{enumerate}

The interface will not infer missing disturbances from graph position.
Node $B$ and edge $S$ or its complete primitive pair will be required.
An explicit zero disturbance will be distinguished from an unavailable
observation. If both direct $S$ and an $L/C$ pair are provided for an edge,
the final contract will reject the ambiguous representation. Capacity
must be positive, load nonnegative, and units compatible.

Input violations will produce a reasoned validation failure instead of
silently clipping malformed inputs. Clipping will apply only to the
declared susceptibility mapping. Floating-point behavior near zero and
saturation will be checked against high-precision worked cases. The
engine will report the numerical tolerance used by its tests and ranking
policy; any change to the arithmetic implementation will require
regression comparison with the specified equations.

\subsubsection{Prototype Integration and Visualization}

The prototype will retain three layers: a React/Vite interface with directed
graph visualization, a FastAPI validation service, and the versioned
GBBRPM evaluation engine. The preliminary implementation used the
\texttt{gbbrpm} v0.1.0 package; the evaluated proposal implementation
will pin and record its actual version or revision.

\input{figures/fig_prototype_architecture}

Synthetic and user-imported modes will pass the same validated schema to
the engine. An operational adapter will remain inactive until a suitable,
provenance-complete dataset is available. The interface will support
parameter inspection and editing, explicit execution, graph inspection,
rankings, and incoming-contribution traces.

Each evaluation response will also carry the topological order computed by
the evaluation service and an ordered evaluation trace. A trace step will
declare its type as source, transfer, or aggregate, together with the
receiving node, that node's local disturbance $B$ and computed risk state
$R$, and, for a transfer step, the corresponding edge contribution $Q$.
Trace steps will be assembled
directly from quantities already computed by the engine; the service will not
re-evaluate the propagation rule during trace generation, ensuring that
displayed steps cannot diverge from reported results. The trace serves as
a presentation record of a single evaluation rather than a redundant
re-computation.

The interface will expose the analytical quantities required for that check.
For a selected node, it will report relation susceptibility and incoming
contributions alongside the aggregation expression of Equation~(3-4); for a
selected relation, it will display the numerical substitution of
Equation~(3-2); and for an imported network lacking physical parameters $L$
and $C$, the explicit susceptibility $S_{ij}$ will remain editable under
the same unit-interval bounds as adapted edges. Selection will be
coordinated between the ranking table and the graph canvas, so that
choosing a reported rank position highlights the corresponding node and
a rank and its topological realization are examined concurrently.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Trace Presentation}

Presentation of a completed evaluation will be optional and disabled by
default. When enabled, the trace will be presented after execution, and a
stored trace can be replayed without issuing a further evaluation
request. Because presentation operates on a returned result, it adds no
computation and cannot alter numerical values.

Replay will follow a deterministic schedule compiled once from the trace.
Rather than presenting the evaluation as a strictly sequential listing,
the schedule will pipeline it: a node will begin receiving contributions
as soon as its earliest predecessor has settled, so that independent
branches advance concurrently under a single clock. Each contribution
will traverse its edge only after its source has settled, and a node's
displayed state will converge and be revealed only after all of its
incoming contributions have arrived. The presentation will thus respect
the topological evaluation order computed by the service as a dependency
constraint while permitting concurrent branches to interleave in display
order. Arriving contributions will be rendered as they traverse their
edges, and the event boundaries of the schedule will establish discrete
transition marks to which playback can seek. While a step is displayed,
the interface will report the edge contribution defined by
Equation~(3-2) and the partially aggregated state defined by
Equation~(3-4), in each case converging on the value returned by the
engine. The ranking derived from the evaluation will remain withheld
until the replay completes or is explicitly skipped, and will be released
when playback is terminated, as when a parameter is modified, the dataset
is changed, or presentation is disabled. The withholding is confined to
the ranking panel, so intermediate quantities are examined before final
outputs are read without restricting the comparison and export views.

Replay will be interruptible and reproducible: modifying a parameter or
changing the dataset will discard active playback, whereas restoring a
stored replay will reproduce the identical schedule and values. Continuous
motion will be suppressed, and replay will initialize in a paused state,
when the environment declares a reduced-motion preference; the underlying
schedule, event boundaries, and reported values remain unaffected. The
reporting panel will render as an overlay layered over the visualization
region so that its size cannot perturb the graph layout or trigger canvas
reflows, and its vertical dimension will be adjustable by either pointer
or keyboard within that constraint.

Presentation timing will remain presentational. Sequential, concurrent,
or layered arrangement will not be interpreted as physical propagation
speed, and no presentation option will alter the engine's topological
computation or numerical outputs.

\subsection{Data Sources, DAG Construction, and Parameterization}
\label{domain-parameterization}

Each domain will be documented using the same template: source and version;
node and edge meanings; direction and acyclicity; local disturbance;
susceptibility and transmission; missingness or excluded records; and
permitted output interpretation. Observed, documented, derived, assumed,
synthetic, and simulated fields will be labeled separately.

\subsubsection{Drainage Instantiation}

Nodes and directed relations will represent the chosen drainage entities
and permitted downstream influence. A load-to-capacity adapter will use
\[
u_{ij}=\frac{L_{ij}}{C_{ij}},\qquad S_{ij}=\min(1,u_{ij}),
\quad L_{ij}\ge0,\ C_{ij}>0.
\eqtag{3-7}
\]
Local $B_i$ values will represent the declared disturbance scenario or
documented condition mapping. Synthetic inputs will not be called
operational measurements. Operational records will be sought for topology,
flow direction, specifications, loading/capacity, inspection and
maintenance history, and relevant outcomes. Their adequacy will be
assessed before any field-validation claim is made.

SWMM scenarios will supply an external hydraulic reference. They will be
aligned to the corresponding graph, intervention, and node mapping.
Acyclic downstream influence does not represent hydraulic backwater,
surcharge, ponding, or redistribution. Simulated reference outputs will
remain distinct from field observations.

\subsubsection{Software-Dependency Instantiation}

Packages will represent nodes, with edges directed from a dependency to
its dependent so that modeled influence reaches the consuming package.
The preliminary Express 4.18.2 case has 71 packages and 128 edges.
Version-specific advisory evidence and the declared severity mapping
will provide $B_i$; severity categories will be treated as bounded index
inputs rather than calibrated exploit probabilities.

The initial case will retain $S_{ij}=1$ and neutral baseline transmission.
The uniform sensitivity sweep will use
$\tau\in\{0,0.25,0.50,0.75,1.00\}$.
The topology and advisory snapshots will be versioned. Cyclic dependency
input will be rejected by the DAG engine; any separately proposed
component-condensation approach would require its own node semantics.

Shared \texttt{qs} and \texttt{send} paths in the preliminary Express
case will be identified as reconvergence rather than independent sources.
The case will test mapping and parameter response, not future incident
prediction.

\subsubsection{Electrical Instantiation}

The electrical workflow will separate full-network import verification
from the observable Component 2 mechanism study using the authorized
Caltech SoCal 28-Bus data \citep{xie2025}. The preliminary full import
contains 187 bus records and 177 active connections; import counts alone
will not establish physical acyclicity or fault prediction.

Component 2 will be selected using observability, authorized topology,
traceable meters, and DAG compatibility. Zero-impedance-connected buses
will be consolidated using the documented adapter. The preliminary complete
component has 13 nodes and 12 elements; exclusion of an unobserved element
leaves a 12-node, 11-edge arborescence. This fit-for-purpose case will not be
treated as a representative sample of distribution grids.

Synchronized phasors will provide real and reactive loading.
For $T$ aligned snapshots, downstream powers will first be aggregated
within each snapshot, then converted to apparent power:
\[
L_{ij}=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\sqrt{P_{ij,t}^{\,2}+Q_{ij,t}^{\,2}}.
\eqtag{3-8}
\]
The magnitude of mean $P$ and $Q$ will not be substituted for this mean
of magnitudes. Transformer capacity will follow the documented nameplate
policy; three-phase line capacity in kVA will be
$3V_{\mathrm{phase}}I_{\mathrm{rated}}/1000$
for voltage in volts and current in amperes. Equation~(3-7) will then
supply electrical susceptibility.

The source and terminal-meter assignments, synchronization tolerance,
unusable meters, neighboring-feeder exclusion, and unallocated residual
power will be recorded. The preliminary calculation uses 24 normal-operation
snapshots. Declared disturbances will be injected for controlled scenarios;
they will not be relabeled as observed faults. Mains-register unit
inconsistencies will be documented, with phasor-derived loading retained
as the selected input.

Independent switching-event analysis will be conditional on event-aligned
measurements and outcomes. Existing normal-operation coverage does not
provide those outcomes. Restricted raw measurements and credentials will
not be included in the public manuscript package.

\subsection{Properties, Sensitivity, Robustness, and Dependence}
\label{behavioral-evaluation}

Analytical arguments will be complemented by implementation tests for
zero disturbance, isolated nodes, chains, independent convergence,
reconvergence, zero edge modifiers, and saturated local disturbance.
Randomized admissible DAG/input cases will test the implementation against
the declared properties. Passing finitely many tests will not replace
the analytical argument.

Sensitivity tests will vary severity, source location, susceptibility,
uniform transmission, intermediate disturbance, and source combinations,
holding unrelated inputs fixed. Outputs will include node and terminal
states, maximum and mean risk, risk sum, and node counts above an
explicit reporting cutoff. A cutoff will be an analytical choice rather
than a physical failure threshold.

The planned robustness protocol will use 600 trials at each relative
perturbation magnitude $\delta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ with seed 41.
For this protocol, each edge susceptibility will be independently perturbed
as $S'_{ij}=\min(1,\max(0,S_{ij}(1+\epsilon_{ij})))$,
where $\epsilon_{ij}$ is uniform on $[-\delta,\delta]$.
Topology, disturbances, and transmission will remain fixed.
This stated protocol will be versioned; earlier outputs will not be
assumed identical if their generation conventions differ.

The dependence diagnostic will compare ordinary aggregation with the
preliminary ancestry-aware path-pruned counterfactual. Overlapping source
lineages will be grouped and their strongest incoming contribution retained;
nonoverlapping groups will retain complement-product aggregation.
Differences will quantify sensitivity to that particular diagnostic
construction. The counterfactual will not be presented as ground truth
or an independently validated dependence solution.

\subsection{Computational and Input-Cost Assessment}
\label{computational-scalability}

The lightweight claim will be assessed as a resource and representation
tradeoff. With adjacency-list storage and constant-cost edge operations,
DAG checking, topological ordering, and propagation require
\[
O(|V|+|E|),
\quad\text{whereas complete comparison sorting adds }O(|V|\log|V|).
\eqtag{3-9}
\]
Graph and stored contribution-trace memory will be $O(|V|+|E|)$.
The claim applies to one scenario with prepared inputs. Domain adapters,
data retrieval, many repeated scenarios, and interface rendering have
additional costs.

Sparse DAG sizes will initially include
$|V|\in\{20,50,100,250,500,1000\}$ with recorded edge counts, followed
by a denser-series stress test at 20, 50, 100, and 250 nodes with
$|E|=\lfloor0.25|V|(|V|-1)/2\rfloor$. A seeded random topological
ordering will restrict all edges to forward pairs, and unique pairs
will be selected up to the declared edge count. Sparse cases will target
three edges per node. Graphs and bounded inputs will be fixed by seed
41. Five warm-up runs and 30 measured repetitions per
configuration will provide median, interquartile range, and raw timings.
Graph validation/order, propagation, ranking, and total engine evaluation
will be timed separately, alongside peak memory under a stated measurement
boundary. Hardware, operating system, language, library versions, and
timer/memory instrumentation will be recorded.

The same prepared inputs will be used for relevant local, uniform, and
structural comparison implementations. Simpler baselines may remain
cheaper. A required-input inventory will separately list observed and
derived quantities, adapter effort, and missingness requirements.
A smaller core will not imply easy data acquisition.

Higher-fidelity comparison will document the modeled quantities, omitted
mechanisms, required fields, and workflow costs. A quantitative SWMM speed
claim will be made only if a separately documented paired benchmark is
performed with declared scenarios, simulation duration, solver settings,
hardware, and timing boundaries. A hydraulic simulation and a static
comparative-index evaluation remain different tasks.

\subsection{Separate Domain-Case Evaluation}\label{domain-evaluation}

The drainage case will compare scenario-induced priority changes with
SWMM maximum-depth and flooding changes under the aligned intervention
protocol. Spearman association, priority-set overlap, and the share of
positive hydraulic worsening outside the downstream region will be
reported. Negative or low agreement will inform the limits of the graph
index rather than be converted into evidence of validated utility.

The software case will report root and network states, root rank, and
ranking agreement across the uniform transmission sweep. Advisory-derived
disturbance is an input, not an independent incident label. A high root
state may include shared-path influence, whose contribution is not
quantified merely by stable ranking.

The observable electrical case will propose reruns of the preliminary
43-scenario configuration: one zero-disturbance reference; four root
severity levels; disturbance at each of 12 nodes; five load scales; five
transmission levels; 15 pairs of internal sources; and one all-internal
case. Node and terminal risk, risk sums, and affected-node counts will
be reported. The arborescence tests local-plus-incoming aggregation and
branch coverage, not downstream reconvergence.

Cross-case synthesis will examine defensible mappings, DAG eligibility,
core-rule consistency, computational requirements, and evidence limits.
Domain scores, correlations, and physical outcomes will not be pooled
as interchangeable performance measures. A common equation does not
establish common calibration.

\subsection{Prototype Functional Verification}\label{prototype-verification}

The prototype will be checked against the versioned engine for fixture
inventory, reproduction of frozen reference states within declared
tolerance, valid explicit-susceptibility imports, and rejection of unknown
endpoints, duplicate records, NaN and negative-infinity inputs, invalid
bounds, and cycles.

Verification will treat the prototype as a presentation layer over the
frozen engine, testing agreement rather than re-deriving results. The
returned trace will be evaluated across all benchmark fixtures (N1--N5)
for determinism and strict correspondence with reported engine outputs:
it must account for each node and each edge exactly once; each step must
reproduce the reported local disturbance $B$ and state $R$ of its node;
transfer steps must reference already-evaluated sources in topological order;
and each step's classification must correspond to the presence of incoming
contributions. Selected receiving states will be reconstructed algebraically
from displayed contributions as an independent check on trace completeness.

The replay schedule will be verified for settlement causality, which
requires that no node's displayed state be revealed before every incoming
contribution has landed and that each contribution begin only after its
source has settled; for strictly ordered event boundaries; for valid
concurrency of independent branches; and for deterministic execution that
does not mutate its input. Two invariance conditions will be tested
explicitly: (1)~numerical results
and the final ranking must be identical across display modes and with
presentation disabled, and (2)~displayed quantities must agree with the
engine's values at settled points, namely once a contribution has arrived
and once a node has aggregated all of its incoming contributions; values
between these points are presentational interpolation and are not held to
this condition.

A production frontend build and the parameter-edit-and-run workflow will
be checked. Presentation geometry will be inspected for containment
within the visualization region under both pointer and keyboard
adjustment. Interactive graph transitions, selection correspondence, and
responsive layout will be verified manually in a browser because automated
headless tests do not evaluate interactive canvas rendering. These checks
will assess implementation consistency only; usability, accessibility,
security, multi-user operation, persistence, and deployment hardening will
require separate evaluation if added to scope.

\subsection{Analysis, Evidence Synthesis, and Reproducibility}
\label{analysis-reproducibility}

\subsubsection{Metrics and Numerical Reporting}

Paired scenario differences will be reported at node and network level.
Spearman correlation will use average ranks for tied standing; a constant
rank vector will be reported as undefined rather than assigned a favorable
correlation. Numerical tie grouping will use a declared absolute tolerance
of $10^{-12}$, with each descending group's highest score as its anchor.

Top-$k$ sets will use the nominal cutoff
\[
    k = \min(3,|V|)\quad\text{or}\quad
    k = \max(1,\lceil 0.20|V|\rceil),
\]
depending on the declared metric. All scores within the numerical
tolerance of the cutoff score will be included. The resulting set can
exceed nominal $k$. Jaccard similarity will be
$J(A,B)=|A\cap B|/|A\cup B|$.
Top-three and top-20\% measures will be labeled separately.
These conventions will be applied consistently to planned runs and
will not be retroactively attributed to every historical table.

Boundary and analytical tests will assess formal correctness; comparator
differences will assess added information dependence; resource measures
will assess cost; and domain analyses will support only their declared
case-specific claims. Output disagreement alone will not establish
better prioritization.

\subsubsection{Provenance and Acquisition Contingencies}

The record will include input hashes, source versions, engine revision,
scenario settings, random seeds, trial counts, environment, and output
files. Reconstructed historical inputs will be distinguished from their
lost original raw dataset. The software topology and electrical measured
loading will remain real-data inputs despite the preliminary status of
their experiments.

Previously examined scenarios will remain exploratory. Any new
confirmatory evaluation will specify its criteria and independent data
before inspecting outcomes. If suitable agency records or event-aligned
electrical evidence are unavailable at the acquisition checkpoint, the
study will report that limitation and review the permitted scope with the
adviser. Missing field records will not be replaced with invented
operational observations.
